# Questions for the doctor (read aloud)

Read the plain line out loud. The "why" line is just for you.
Ask the 7 in part A first. Part B only if there's time.

## Before you ask: what question 4 is about

- In 2010 the health ministry set a goal: a suspected cancer should start treatment within **6 weeks** of the referral ([STM 2010:6, section 7.2](https://www.iccp-portal.org/system/files/plans/selv_2010_6_syovan_hoito_verkko.pdf)). THL's 2016 guide on measuring cancer waits ([Ohjaus 21/2016](https://www.julkari.fi/items/0424a55a-f159-406f-8883-5dfdad4d6772)) sets how to measure it; we could not open its full text.
- It's only guidance, not law. The law's deadlines are 3 weeks to start assessing, 3 months to finish, 6 months to treat, and none is for cancer ([Health Care Act §52](https://www.finlex.fi/fi/lainsaadanto/2010/1326)). So a patient can wait months and every legal deadline is still met.
- Nobody publishes cancer waits against the 6 weeks ([THL](https://thl.fi/en/data-and-statistics/access-to-treatment/access-to-services-in-specialised-health-care)). That's our problem 1.
- What the answer means: "yes, and we check it" means the dates exist and we can use them. "Yes, but nobody checks" is exactly problem 1. "What target?" means our pitch is "a county should adopt it, and our tool tracks it".

## A. Ask these first

**1.** On the public route, where do patients lose the most time? Getting in to see the GP, getting referred, or waiting for tests?
*Follow-up:* Is it any different for people who go through occupational health?
> why: problem 3 and the route gap. We only have one lymphoma study on this.

**2.** Can a health centre GP in your area book a CT scan themselves?
*Follow-up:* How long is the wait for it?
> why: a GP fast lane does nothing if the GP can't order the scan.

**3.** Which dates does the hospital system actually record? Say, referral received, first specialist visit, diagnosis, team decision, start of treatment.
> why: problem 1. Tells us if a county wait view can run on real dates.

**4.** Do you still work to the six-week target, from a suspected cancer referral to treatment?
*Follow-up:* Does anyone check it?
> why: problem 1. See the box above.

**5.** An OECD report says Finland fines providers that miss wait targets. Has that ever happened for cancer?
> why: a judge might bring it up. We think it's only the general care guarantee.

**6.** Pirkanmaa records stage way more often than everyone else. Why?
*Follow-up:* Did it help patients, or just the data?
> why: problem 4. Their reports went from 42% to 80%. No patient result published.

**7.** Would you use a cancer-risk prompt in Apotti, Omni360 or Lifecare?
*Follow-up:* How many a day before you'd start ignoring them?
> why: our GP prompt. Too many alerts and doctors click past them.

## B. If there's time

**Pathway**

8. Does your county have a fast track for suspected cancer from the health centre?
9. A small study found the public route took about 50 days longer for lymphoma. Would that hold for lung, bowel or breast cancer too?
10. Does anyone record how a patient came in? Health centre, occupational health or private?
11. How often do hospitals send a GP referral back?

**Stage**

12. Who sends the clinical report to the Cancer Registry?
    *Follow-up:* Is stage usually in the notes and just not sent, or not written down at all?
13. Could stage be filled in on a short form at the tumour board meeting, like they do in Belgium?

**GP tools**

14. Would a national list of warning symptoms help GPs more than a risk prompt?
15. If a GP could book a CT straight from a risk prompt, would that save real time, or just move the queue?
16. Do you use FIT or CA-125 tests when a patient comes in with symptoms?
17. Catalonia gives GPs one referral form and a named hospital for each health centre. Would that work here?

**Using it**

18. If we showed a county a map of cancer waits, who would actually act on it?
    *Follow-up:* What would they need to trust it?
19. Is there any table of waits, stage or referrals by county you could share or point us to this weekend?

**Equity**

20. Can you think of one example of the same cancer treated differently in two counties?
21. People with less education get surgery less often, 36% against 51%. What do you think is behind that?
