Prepared 2026-11-14 for the Junction 2026 AstraZeneca cancer-care track. Status: no testimony yet. Context: [00-discovery-brief.md](00-discovery-brief.md). "Deck p.10" means page 10 of the Kuitunen insights deck (8-step delay model, see [notes](../tracks/astrazeneca-insights-deck.md)). "RQ" = research question.

## 0. Ground rules (all interviews)

- **Opening, in your own words:** "Thanks for talking with us. We're a hackathon team looking at how people get to cancer care in Finland. I'm not a clinician, and this isn't a sales call. There are no right answers. You can skip any question or stop at any time. Is it OK if I take notes without your name?" Ask for recording separately, only if they say yes.
- Ask about what **actually happened**, not what they would do. Keep it 15–20 minutes.
- Don't pitch. Don't mention any AstraZeneca product or our ideas (glasses, dashboards).
- Tag each case with the step where it slowed, using deck p.10: 1 symptoms begin · 2 deciding to seek care · 3 first contact · 4 physician assessment · 5 tests and referral · 6 diagnosis · 7 team plan · 8 treatment start.
- **Current symptom?** If someone describes an unresolved symptom now, say you can't advise. Suggest their GP or the out-of-hours line (116 117), or 112 if urgent. Note that you did this.

## 1. Mentors (Medical Affairs, Diagnostics)

- **Who:** Kaisa Kaitila (Medical Affairs, on site Sat daytime); Hanna Toivola (Diagnostics, listed Fri 18:00 only, see gaps). Mentors work for AstraZeneca, a company with a commercial interest. Treat their input as expert opinion, not neutral data.
- **RQs:** RQ1 At which of the 8 steps do public-route patients lose most time, and why? RQ2 Who owns that delay? RQ3 Which county or GP tools exist for that step? RQ4 What data exists about it, and who holds it?
- **Core prompts:**
  1. "Tell me about the last patient or case you know of where the route to diagnosis was slow. Start from the first symptom."
  2. "What happened next? What tools, forms or workarounds did people use?"
  3. "Where did it fall short, and what happened as a result: for the patient, the referral, the start of treatment?"
- **Follow-ups:** "Which step was that?" · "Public route or private/occupational?" · "Who made the next decision?" · "Roughly how long between those points?" · "What data would have shown this earlier?" · "Did you see this yourself, or hear it second-hand? From whom?"
- **Do not ask:** patient details (name, age, town, hospital, diagnosis date) · AstraZeneca strategy, pipeline or unpublished data · "Which AZ drug should we use?" · "Would AZ buy this?" · "Is the public system broken?" (leading)

## 2. People with a cancer diagnosis, or who supported a relative

- **Who:** team members' networks and other participants. Patients and relatives both count; tag relatives as **proxy** reports.
- **RQs:** RQ1 Where between symptom, first contact and referral did it slow down? RQ2 What workarounds did people use (work health care, private care, paying, asking contacts)? RQ3 Where did it fall short, and what did that cost them?
- **Core prompts:**
  1. "Tell me about the last time you, or your relative, noticed a symptom that worried you. What happened, step by step?"
  2. "What did you do to get seen? Who did you contact, what did you use, and did you pay for anything or use work or private care?"
  3. "Where did that fall short, and what happened as a result? How long did it take, and what did it cost?"
- **Follow-ups:** "What made you act that day?" · "What happened right after?" · "How long from the first call to the first appointment?" · "Did anyone help you find your way through? Who?" · "Did money, time off work or travel make a difference?" · "What would have made that go better?" · "Can you say that in your own words?"
- **Do not ask:** "Did you get the right diagnosis?" · "Was your doctor at fault?" · "Do you think the system is unfair?" · "What stage was it?" (ask only if volunteered) · "Would you use an app that…?" · "How much would you pay?"

## 3. Primary care doctor or nurse (if reachable)

- **Who:** a GP or practice nurse, via networks or mentors. One case is enough. No contact yet.
- **RQs:** RQ1 Where does a suspected cancer case stall between first visit and referral (deck steps 3–5)? RQ2 Which tools and forms do they use? RQ3 Do they hear back about the referral, and how?
- **Core prompts:**
  1. "Tell me about the last patient you suspected might have cancer. What made you suspicious?"
  2. "What did you do next? Who did you refer to, how, and which forms or systems did you use?"
  3. "Where did it fall short? Did you hear back, how long did it take, and what happened to the patient in the meantime?"
- **Follow-ups:** "How long from first visit to referral?" · "What made the referral hard, if it was?" · "Who else had to be involved?" · "What do you see but can't follow up?" · "What would have helped in that case?"
- **Do not ask:** patient identifiers · for copies of letters or records · "Would you use a referral tool?" · "Do you think GPs are overloaded?" (leading; ask "What was that week like?")

## 4. Direction B: higher lifestyle-risk groups (short)

- **Who:** adults who have tried to cut back on smoking, drinking or unhealthy eating, including lower-income or lower-education groups. Recruit through community or networks. Record income or education only as broad groups.
- **RQs:** RQ1 What helped, and what fell short, when they tried to change a habit? RQ2 Did cost, time or access decide whether they tried?
- **Core prompts:**
  1. "Tell me about the last time you tried to cut back on smoking, drinking or eating. What happened, from the moment you decided?"
  2. "What did you use: people, apps, services, medicines, money? What did it cost you in money or time?"
  3. "Where did it fall short, and what happened as a result?"
- **Follow-ups:** "What made you decide that day?" · "Who did you ask for help?" · "Did a health service help?" · "What made it easier, or harder?"
- **Do not ask:** "Would you wear smart glasses / use a coach?" · "What features would you want?" · "Would you pay €X?" · anything that sounds like judging their habits.

## 6. Consent and ethics

Taking part is voluntary. Get verbal consent at the start, and do not record without a separate yes. Participants can skip any question or stop at any time. Notes use codes only. Do not write names, dates of birth, exact dates, hospital or clinic names, postcodes, employers, or any medical record content. Record cancer type only if volunteered, and only as a broad group. Mentors must not share patient-level detail even if they offer it. The challenge allows only public, aggregate data, so no patient-level data goes into the project or the repo. Raw notes stay out of the repo and are deleted after judging, unless the team decides otherwise. This is a hackathon, not a formal ethics review, so every finding stays a hypothesis.
