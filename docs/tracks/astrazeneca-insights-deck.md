# AstraZeneca track – insights deck notes

Source: `astrazeneca-insights-deck-2026-11-13.pdf` (this folder), "Equitable Cancer Care – How Can We Ensure It in Finland", Hanne Kuitunen (oncologist, Service Area Director, Oulu University Hospital / Pohde, North Ostrobothnia wellbeing county). Shown at Junction, Espoo, 13.11.2026. 16 slides. Page numbers below are PDF pages. Speaker declares consulting for AstraZeneca and others (p. 2).

## Main question (p. 6)

> "Do two patients with the same cancer in Finland have an equal chance of recovery, regardless of where they live, how much they earn, their educational background, or their ability to navigate the healthcare system?"

## Key points

| Page | Point |
| --- | --- |
| 3–4 | Reform goal: right patient, right setting, right treatment, timely care. Regional and socioeconomic gaps remain; counties develop services at different speeds; different service networks, digital tools and pathways "lead or enable inequities"; limited resources. |
| 5 | **Parallel systems:** public, occupational, private and student healthcare. Employment, insurance and money give faster routes. Bottlenecks in public care (assessment, diagnosis, treatment). **Inverse care law:** access may be least where need is greatest. Out-of-pocket costs (fees, medicines, travel) delay tests, cut medicine use, discourage screening. Gaps for vulnerable groups. |
| 6 | Relative survival by sex is similar; by education it differs (Finnish Cancer Registry 2024). |
| 7 | Inequality accumulates along the whole pathway: prevention → screening → symptom recognition → access → treatment → follow-up → rehab/palliative. Lower SES: later stage, fewer curative/systemic treatments, lower screening and trial participation. Inequalities are "structural rather than voluntary". Drivers: lifestyle, environment, screening, **health literacy**, access (barriers, delays, **navigation**), treatment/rehab. |
| 8 | Prevention: ~half of cancers may be preventable. Lifestyle risks cluster in lower SES. Tobacco >2000 new cases/yr. Alcohol: no safe level, no clear SES difference. "Effective prevention requires policies and services that reach disadvantaged populations." |
| 9 | Screening (breast, colorectal, cervical): high overall, lower in low SES; migrant, Roma, sexual and gender minority barriers; language matters. |
| 10 | **Delay model, 8 steps:** 1 symptoms begin, 2 considering seeking care (patient delay: fear, minimising, low health literacy), 3 first contact, 4 physician assessment, 5 tests and referrals, 6 diagnosis, 7 MDT plan, 8 treatment start (system delay: symptoms not recognised, limited GP access, waiting lists, fragmented pathways, regional differences). "Identify and reduce delays at every stage." |
| 11 | **DLBCL (lymphoma) example, Kuitunen et al. Cancer Medicine 2026;15:e71920:** days from symptom onset, public primary care vs private/occupational: first contact 72 vs 42, physician visit 94 vs 43, referral 147 vs 77, specialist 204 vs 118, **treatment start 207 vs 123**. Tokola et al. Cancer Medicine 2025;14(22): symptom-to-diagnosis >7 weeks linked to worse progression-free survival. |
| 12–13 | Nordic review (Acta Oncologica 2022;61(11):1317–1331, 98 studies): lower SES → later stage, less active treatment, worse survival. |
| 14–15 | Zai et al. BMJ Public Health 2025;3:e002829, Finland 2000–2019, n=469,522: higher vs lower education got surgery 51% vs 36%, radiotherapy 25% vs 14%, chemo 24% vs 15%; **cancer mortality 27% vs 47%**. Lower education: more cancer, later stage, less surgery. |
| 16 | Costs €1.5bn (2023) → €2.6bn (2035). "How can we ensure equity when not every treatment can be offered to everyone?" → "consistent, evidence-based criteria for treatment selection across Finland." |

## What this means for us

- The strongest, quantified gap is **public vs private/occupational route**, mostly in the early steps (first contact → referral). Most of it happens before the specialist, in primary care and patient delay.
- **Navigation and health literacy** are named drivers. This fits a patient- or GP-facing tool better than a county dashboard.
- Direction B (prevention) is supported by p. 8, but only if it reaches **lower-SES groups**; a gadget for wealthy early adopters would widen the gap.
- Unverified: we have not read the three papers themselves yet. **Now read (2026-11-14):** see corrections below and `docs/discovery/04-cause-map.md`.

## Corrections after reading the papers (2026-11-14)

| Page | Slide says | Corrected wording |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 9 | Screening lower in low SES (no numbers) | 2024 national screening: colorectal 73.5%, cervical 73.5%, breast 81.6%. Education split not found in any source; do not show it until sourced. |
| 11 (left) | DLBCL times show SES gap | The figure's times match the paper. The paper's test is by route (primary care vs private/occupational), not by education or income: median 127 vs 76 days, p=0.027, n=48 vs 39. Education, income and occupation were not significant for the delay. Kuitunen et al. was Incyte-funded. |
| 11 (right) | Symptom-to-diagnosis >7 wks linked to worse PFS | Tokola et al. (same 160-patient design, 7 hospitals, Kuitunen a co-author): the 7-week cut-off is data-driven and unadjusted. The PFS hazard ratio (5.89) applies only to the high Ki-67 subgroup. Whole cohort: 2-year PFS 89% vs 74% (p=0.012). |
| 11 | "Two papers" | The two DLBCL papers are one cohort design; count as one source. |
| 15 | Cancer mortality 27% vs 47% | 27% and 47% are crude shares of people who died of cancer. Headline the adjusted cancer-death hazard ratio: 1.13 (1.10–1.16) men, 1.14 (1.11–1.17) women, low vs high education. |
| 15 | Lower education → later stage | Direction confirmed; about 30% of cases have no stage recorded. |
| 14 | n = 469,522 "patients" | These are cancer cases (240,746 men, 228,776 women). |
| 14 | Surgery 51 vs 36% etc. | Crude shares, not adjusted. |
