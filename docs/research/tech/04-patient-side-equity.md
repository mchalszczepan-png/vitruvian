# 04 Patient-side and equity tech (Finland, cancer entry)

Scope: patient-facing triage, symptom checkers, booking, navigation apps, and digital-exclusion evidence. Built for P3 (no fast lane for suspected cancer in public primary care, seen through the two-speed-entry lens) and P1 (nobody measures total wait from first symptom to treatment).

Date of research: 2026-11-14. Tags: READ = page opened, SNIPPET = search result text only, UNVERIFIED = memory or not checked, [source needed] = no source found after search.

## Summary

1. The Finnish tools that exist (Omaolo, Klinik, Helsinki Maisa/chat) give advice, sorting or booking. None gives a fast lane for suspected cancer, and none measures symptom-to-treatment time.
2. The best Finnish validation ([Omaolo, 877 cases](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11467609/)) had no cancer symptom among its 15 chief symptoms, and it excluded people who could not finish the tool because of technology problems. The people we care about are missing from the evidence.
3. The only cost evidence ([Klinik in Myyrmaki, Aalto](https://www.aalto.fi/en/news/artificial-intelligence-brings-14-savings-on-average-patient-treatment-costs)) shows a 14% drop in cost per patient, not a drop in wait or a change in cancer diagnosis.

### Digital-exclusion evidence (Finland)

- Survey, n = 4,495: older adults at risk of digital exclusion; good digital skills only slow the drop until about age 80 ([SNIPPET, research.fi](https://research.fi/en/results/publication/02111519YJ)).
- Low health literacy linked to not using digital devices ([SNIPPET, journal.fi 2022](https://journal.fi/inf/article/download/122559/72926/252632)).
- Higher age and low education linked to negative attitudes toward web health care ([SNIPPET, JMIR 2021](https://www.jmir.org/2021/9/e28066)).
- City of Helsinki: low education, poor health, memory or vision problems raise digital-exclusion risk ([SNIPPET, kaupunkitieto.hel.fi](https://kaupunkitieto.hel.fi/en/digitalisation-and-accessibility-of-public-services-in-finland-and-helsinki)).

## White space

1. No Finnish checker covers cancer symptoms as a route (SNIPPET: Omaolo list; READ: no cancer symptom in validation).
2. Nobody measures symptom-to-treatment time across routes (READ: Helsinki measures call-back and first-visit waits; Aalto measures cost only).
3. No tool gives a fast lane. All are advice, sorting, booking or post-diagnosis.
4. Omaolo and Klinik have no language, income or literacy split in their outcome data (READ).
5. Validation excluded people who failed on technology (READ). The target group is not in the evidence.
6. Kela cut to EUR 8 (user context; [Kela](https://www.kela.fi/web/selkokieli/korvaukset-sairaanhoidosta-ja-hammashoidosta) lists the private visit fee as SNIPPET). No public data found linking it to who waits longer.
7. Post-diagnosis apps (Belong, Kaiku) exist. Nothing covers the wait before diagnosis.
8. Vendor fragility: Babylon collapse (SNIPPET). Public procurement needs exit terms.
