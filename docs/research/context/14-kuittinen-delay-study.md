# Kuittinen lung cancer delay study (interview)

Checked 2026-11-14 with WebSearch and WebFetch only. WebFetch answers come from a small model, so the key quotes were re-checked with a second, narrower fetch of the same Yle page, and both gave the same numbers. The Finnish MOT broadcast itself was not read. The reference-lookup MCP server returned 429 and was not needed; DOIs were checked on doi.org or PubMed.

## Bottom line

- The study is real. Yle/MOT reports an ongoing, unpublished study coordinated by Outi Kuittinen (professor of oncology, University of Eastern Finland) on lung cancer delays in Kuopio, Tampere, Turku and Rovaniemi.
- The published split does not match the expert's "three equal thirds". The article gives: patient "More than 50 days", primary healthcare 41 days, specialised healthcare 58 days, total 155 days. No "GP runs tests before referral" category appears.
- The interview itself was not found in any search.

## Who is the professor

- **Kuittinen: confirmed.** Yle: "An ongoing study coordinated by Outi Kuittinen, professor of oncology at the University of Eastern Finland" (https://yle.fi/a/74-20251023, opened).
- **Hanne Kuitunen: not the lead.** She is co-author (7th of 10) on the 2025 Acta Oncologica paper that Kuittinen wrote as last author (verified on the page). That makes her a collaborator, not the person in the delay study. Her job title (Oulu University Hospital CMO) comes from a search summary only, unverified.
- **Joonas Kuitunen:** first author of the 2026 DLBCL paper (search snippet, not opened). Different person from Hanne.

## Numbers and wording (Yle/MOT article, English text)

| Item | Value | Exact wording (English original) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Total, Finland | 155 days | "the average time from the onset of symptoms to treatment in Finland is approximately five months" ... "or 155 days" |
| Patient share | "More than 50" days | "More than 50 days of that average delay is attributed to the patient" |
| Primary healthcare | 41 days | "41 days to primary healthcare" |
| Specialised healthcare | 58 days | Stated in one fetch and in a search summary; the sentence was not isolated |
| Sweden / Denmark | 107 / 67 days | "The corresponding total delay is 107 days in Sweden and 67 days in Denmark, according to MOT." |
| Sample, years | Not stated | No sample size or study years in the article |
| Regions | Kuopio, Tampere, Turku, Rovaniemi | Named in search summaries and the article; the article body was not read in full |

- Sum check: 50 + 41 + 58 = 149, not 155. The "more than 50" wording makes the patient share a lower bound, so about 6 days are unexplained.
- Shares of 155: patient about 32% or more, primary healthcare about 26%, specialised about 37%. The expert's "one third each" is roughly right for patient and specialised care, not for primary care.
- The data are described as unpublished. A Lung Cancer Europe page calls the findings "preliminary and not yet peer-reviewed" (https://oncodaily.com/voices/lung-cancer-europe-609186, opened).

## GPs and CT scans

- The Yle/MOT article contains no mention of CT (TT-kuvaus / tietokonetomografia) and does not say whether GPs (yleislääkäri) can order CT in Finland. Checked by a narrow fetch: no sentence with "CT" found.
- Kuittinen's only related point: "faster access to scans". Unconfirmed whether this concerns GP referral rules.
- A Finnish search for GP CT ordering rules found no usable Finnish source. Rules likely vary by wellbeing services county (hyvinvointialue); not verified.

## Published work by Kuittinen

1. **Acta Oncologica 2025, lung cancer surgery (not a delay study).** DOI 10.2340/1651-226X.2025.43398 (opened via doi.org redirect). Volume 64 (2025), pages 909-916, published 2025-07-23. Authors in order: Paappanen V, Tikka J, Jukkola A, Klaavuniemi T, Sailas L, Tengström M, Kuitunen H, Riekkinen T, Tiainen S, Kuittinen O (last). Key sentence: "A total of 156 patients met the staging criteria, of whom 77 underwent surgery." The page gives 545 patients without a year in the text read; a search snippet says 2018. The search summary says the authors link delayed diagnosis to lower surgery rates; not verified in the text.
   Link: https://medicaljournalssweden.se/actaoncologica/article/view/43398 (opened).
2. **Cancer Medicine 2026, DLBCL lymphoma (not lung).** DOI 10.1002/cam4.71920, volume 15, e71920. 3. **Cancer Medicine 2025, DLBCL survival (not lung).** "Symptom-to-diagnosis delay and DLBCL survival", first author Tokola S, PMID 41275414 (PMID = PubMed ID), DOI 10.1002/cam4.71409.
