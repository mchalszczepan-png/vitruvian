# What others tried to cut cancer diagnostic delay and inequity

Date: 2026-11-14. Scope: Denmark, Norway, Sweden, UK, US, Finland. Research only.

Words used:
- **Delay** = time from first symptom or referral to diagnosis or treatment.
- **Stage** = how far the cancer had spread when found (stage IV = most advanced).
- **SES** = socioeconomic status (income, education, deprivation).
- **DOI** = permanent ID of a paper. "Checked" = DOI found in a metadata database. "Snippet" = seen only in a search summary, not read in full.
- **Confidence** = how sure we are of the evidence (high / medium / low).
- **[source needed]** = no source found after two searches. The claim is kept for now.

## Lessons in one view

Our reading:

1. Fast-track referral rules speed the first steps. The treatment-start step stays slow (Norway: [Nilssen 2022](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12885-022-09306-9); Sweden: [Borg 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-46757-x); Denmark: [source needed]).
2. Navigation helps the people who would wait longest, so it narrows gaps. It has not been shown to close them ([Ko 2016](https://doi.org/10.1002/cncr.30109)).
3. Almost no programme measures delay, stage and SES together. The data is usually missing.

## 1. Denmark

### 1a. Cancer patient pathways (CPP, pakkeforløb), 2007–09
- **What:** Organ-specific fast pathways for suspected cancer.
- **Start:** 2007–08 by most descriptions. Exact year not verified. [source needed]
- **Delay:** Jensen et al. 2015, *BMC Cancer*, [DOI 10.1186/s12885-015-1317-7](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12885-015-1317-7) (checked). 12,558 patients. Median diagnostic interval fell from 49 days (before) to 32 days (after). Adjusted change: -17 days (95% CI -19 to -15). Patients in a CPP: -23 days. Patients outside a CPP: -9 days.
- **Stage / survival:** Not reported. The authors say a survival benefit is not well supported.
- **SES / region:** Unadjusted medians similar by income (low 50 to 32 days; high 50 to 31). Adjusted SES results not reported. Gap change not tested.
- **Cost:** Not found.
- **Our reading (why it worked):** Gains came mostly to patients inside a CPP. The authors say CPPs "may thus only seem to expedite" diagnosis for some patients (same paper; quote not re-checked).
- **Confidence:** Medium.

### 1b. Non-specific symptoms pathway (NSSC) and diagnostic centres
- **What:** A GP refers a patient with vague symptoms to a diagnostic centre. Tests run in one place (blood, urine, CT, then visits).
- **Start:** Centres about 2009; NSSC about 2011 (not verified). [source needed] A "2016 wave" claim is from an advocacy source and not verified. [source needed]
- **Delay:** One study reports resolution after about 4 visits within 7 days (cited in [Forster et al. 2018, *Cancer Epidemiology*, DOI 10.1016/j.canep.2018.03.011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.canep.2018.03.011), checked; the underlying study is not identified). No before/after waiting times found.
- **Yield:** Cancer found in 11–21% of referrals across Danish studies ([Forster 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.canep.2018.03.011)). Funen figure is a snippet only. [source needed]
- **Use and outcome:** [Damhus et al. 2022, *BMC Health Services Research*, DOI 10.1186/s12913-022-07545-x](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-022-07545-x) (checked). 513,162 first CT scans, 2013–16. NSSC use: 4.3% after GP referral vs 1.36% after hospital referral. No clear link to lower mortality. Cancer-specific 1-year mortality: 3.0% (GP route) vs 2.3% (hospital route).
- **SES / region:** No SES analysis of the centres found. Regional cancer odds differ (Capital 1.26, North 0.97). [source needed]
- **Cost:** Not found.
- **Our reading (why):** GP triage picks a higher-risk group, so the yield is high. Survival evidence is local, not national.
- **Confidence:** Low.

## 2. Norway: pakkeforløp (from Jan 2015)

- **What:** Cancer pathways for lung, colorectal, breast and prostate cancer from January 2015. Later extended to more cancers.
- **Delay:** [Nilssen et al. 2022, *BMC Cancer*, DOI 10.1186/s12885-022-09306-9](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12885-022-09306-9) (checked by metadata; figures from an earlier snippet, full text not re-read). 20,668 patients, 2015–16. Share meeting the time limit: colon surgery 84%, breast surgery 76%, lung systemic treatment 76%, prostate surgery 43%. Main delay: from decision to start of treatment.
- **Coverage:** About 80% of patients included in a pathway (snippet). [source needed]
- **Single centre:** [Stokstad et al. 2019, *BMC Health Services Research*, DOI 10.1186/s12913-019-4517-z](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-019-4517-z) (checked by metadata; 40% figure from a search summary). 40% of 100 stage I–II lung patients started within 42 days at one centre.
- **Stage / survival:** No national study found.
- **SES / region:** Nilssen 2022 found differences by home area. Travel to a different hospital trust made waits longer. Research Council evaluation (2021, [final report PDF](https://www.regjeringen.no/contentassets/35d9ce1c451a497ca9f25f8c49d5537b/2021-sluttrapport-evaluering-av-pakkeforlop-kreft.pdf)): "conditional success" (betinget suksess), shorter waits, per a search summary of the report. The sample sizes (82 staff, 19 patient interviews) are not confirmed; the PDF could not be opened in this check. [source needed] No SES analysis.
- **Cost:** Not found.
- **Our reading (why it worked or not):** Gains depend on the treatment-start step, which is still slow.
- **Confidence:** Low.

## 3. Sweden: standardiserade vårdförlopp (SVF, from 2015)

- **What:** Standard pathways. Each one sets criteria for suspected cancer and time limits (lead times).
- **Delay:** [Borg et al. 2023, *Scientific Reports*, DOI 10.1038/s41598-023-46757-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-46757-x) (checked). Skåne 2018, 16,596 patients. Average inclusion 74%. Average lead-time target met 47%. Only 5 of 27 pathways met 80%. National 2024: about 45% within lead time (regional compilation; not verified). [source needed]
- **Stage / survival:** Not measured in the sources found.
- **SES / region:** No SES results in Borg 2023. [Smeds & Poksinska, *International Journal of Quality and Service Sciences*, DOI 10.1108/IJQSS-04-2018-0041](https://doi.org/10.1108/IJQSS-04-2018-0041) (checked by metadata; the metadata year is 2018, corrected from 2019): "crowding out". Vulnerable patients may be diagnosed outside the pathway. Riksrevisionen (National Audit Office, RiR 2023:12, [report page](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/betankande/riksrevisionens-rapport-om-statens-arbete-med_hb01sou8/html/); official, not peer-reviewed): state steering through SVF and other waiting-time tools is not effective in many respects (corrected from "on many counts"). It asks for an outcomes study. [source needed]
- **Cost:** SEK 182.5 M paid to regions in Dec 2025 for shorter waits ([government press release](https://www.regeringen.se/pressmeddelanden/2025/12/regeringen-betalar-ut-1825-miljoner-kronor-for-kortare-vantetider-i-cancervarden/)). This is a funding payment, not a full programme cost.
- **Our reading (why it struggled):** Targets are missed. Strict criteria can push out other patients and misplace vulnerable ones.
- **Confidence:** Low to medium.

## 4. UK

### 4a. Two-week wait (2WW), from 2000
- **What:** Fast-track GP referral to a specialist within 2 weeks for suspected cancer.
- **Delay:** No clean estimate found. The share of referrals made by 2WW rose (about 34% by 2013; commentary, not checked). [source needed]
- **Stage / survival:** [Round et al. 2020, *BJGP*, DOI 10.3399/bjgp20x709433](https://doi.org/10.3399/bjgp20x709433) (5-year cohort; checked by metadata). Higher-referring practices had lower odds of late stage (III/IV) for most cancers, but not colorectal. About a third of records had no stage data. Stage numbers not re-read from full text.
- **Background:** [Elliss-Brookes et al. 2012, *British Journal of Cancer*, DOI 10.1038/bjc.2012.408](https://doi.org/10.1038/bjc.2012.408) (checked). Describes 2WW use and outcomes.
- **SES / region:** Not measured clearly.
- **Cost:** Not found.
- **Our reading (why):** It speeds the first visit only. It does not speed tests or treatment after that.
- **Confidence:** Low.

### 4b. Rapid Diagnostic Centres (RDC), from about 2012
- **What:** One-stop clinic for unclear symptoms: tests in one visit.
- **Delay:** [Dolly et al. 2021, *British Journal of Cancer*, DOI 10.1038/s41416-020-01207-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41416-020-01207-7) (checked by metadata). Guy's site, 1,341 referrals, retrospective. About 7% had cancer. Median 28 days to definitive diagnosis.
- **Stage:** Stage IV was the most common stage (40%) among cancers found. No trend shown.
- **SES:** Patients were highly deprived, with 20–25% smokers. No split by deprivation.
- **Other pilots:** [Chapman et al. 2020, *British Journal of Cancer*, DOI 10.1038/s41416-020-0947-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41416-020-0947-y) (checked by metadata). Counts from snippet only, not re-read.
- **Cost:** Not found.
- **Our reading (why):** Quick assessment for unclear symptoms. Cancer is found in a minority of referrals.
- **Confidence:** Low.

### 4c. Faster Diagnosis Standard (FDS)
- **What:** 28-day target from referral to diagnosis or rule-out. Target 75% ([CRUK, Sept 2025](https://news.cancerresearchuk.org/2025/09/11/just-half-of-urgently-referred-cancer-patients-are-diagnosed-on-time/)). CRUK says plans raise the overall target to 80% by March 2026 (plan, not confirmed policy). Reporting from April 2021 [source needed]. Replaced 2WW nationally from October 2023 [source needed].
- **Delay:** Standard reportedly met first time in February 2024 (about 78%). [source needed] Diagnosed cancer patients met the 28-day standard less often over time: 52.3% in April–June 2024, down about four percentage points from 2021 to 2024 (corrected from "57.3% to 52.3%"; [CRUK, Sept 2025](https://news.cancerresearchuk.org/2025/09/11/just-half-of-urgently-referred-cancer-patients-are-diagnosed-on-time/)).
- **Gaps:** A 2024 analysis (source not found) said people in the most deprived areas were 21% less likely to be referred urgently. [source needed] No peer-reviewed split by deprivation found.
- **Confidence:** Low.

### 4d. Patient navigators (Macmillan, NHS)
- **What:** A named person guides the patient through tests and follow-up.
- **Evidence:** No peer-reviewed evaluation of delay, stage or inequity found. Northern Cancer Alliance (2019) report is unpublished. [source needed] It says navigators helped meet waiting-time targets; deprivation findings not confirmed.
- **Confidence:** Low.

## 5. United States

### 5a. Harlem navigation (Freeman)
- **What:** Navigation plus free screening for poor and under-served patients at Harlem Hospital Center.
- **Start:** 1990 (one snippet; not verified). [source needed]
- **Delay:** Retrospective accounts only. A 2006 review ([PMID 16736361](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16736361/); the PubMed page could not be read in this check) is not randomised. No randomised Harlem trial found.
- **Stage:** Early-stage shift claimed for navigation plus screening. Retrospective.
- **SES / race:** Aimed at poor and under-served patients. No stratified result found.
- **Confidence:** Low.

### 5b. Patient Navigation Research Program (PNRP), multi-site, ~2012–14
- **What:** A navigator helps each patient with tests and follow-up after an abnormal screen.
- **Ohio trial:** Group-randomised, 862 patients, 18 clinics. Navigation sped time to diagnostic resolution. [Paskett et al. 2012, *Cancer Epidemiology, Biomarkers & Prevention*, DOI 10.1158/1055-9965.epi-12-0523](https://doi.org/10.1158/1055-9965.epi-12-0523) (checked by metadata).
- **Multi-site trial:** [Freund et al. 2014, *JNCI*, DOI 10.1093/jnci/dju115](https://doi.org/10.1093/jnci/dju115) (checked by metadata; numbers from snippet). No benefit in the first 90 days. Faster resolution from day 91 to 365 (hazard ratio 1.51, 95% CI 1.23–1.84; above 1 = faster).
- **Race and equity:** [Ko et al. 2016, *Cancer*, DOI 10.1002/cncr.30109](https://doi.org/10.1002/cncr.30109) (checked by metadata). 7,514 participants. Median time to resolution without navigation: Black 108 days, non-Hispanic white 65, Hispanic 68. With navigation, Black patients 97 days. Gap narrowed, not closed.
- **Insurance:** No result found.
- **Stage / survival:** Not measured.
- **Cost:** Not found in these sources.
- **Our reading (why it worked):** A named person guides each patient. The effect appears in the later window (day 91 to 365).
- **Confidence:** Medium.

## 6. Finland

### 6a. Existing pathways in the wellbeing counties (since 2023)
- **What:** Each county (hyvinvointialue) runs its own cancer pathways (syöpäpolut).
- **Delay:** No evaluation of waiting times found.
- **Data gap:** A [Yle MOT report (Oct 2025)](https://yle.fi/a/74-20251023) says Finland has no national cancer quality registry for hospital treatment data. The same article says a 400,000 euro cut hits work to build that registry. Cancer societies say there is no national data on treatment delays. [source needed]
- **Stage data:** Stage recorded for 42% of cases nationally in 2024; Pirkanmaa 72%; other areas 33–42% ([Verkkouutiset](https://www.verkkouutiset.fi/a/syopatiedoissa-isot-alueelliset-erot-yksi-seutu-erottuu/)). The date is corrected (corrected: 2024, not "Finnish Cancer Registry news, 17 June 2026"); that 2026 item was not found. [source needed]
- **National Cancer Strategy 2026–2035:** Published by the Ministry of Social Affairs and Health on 10 Nov 2025 ([press release](https://valtioneuvosto.fi/-/1271139/yhteinen-suunta-ihmislahtoiseen-ja-vaikuttavaan-syovan-ehkaisyyn-ja-hoitoon-kansallinen-syopastrategia-on-julkaistu)). - **Money:** FICAN asks €139 M for 2027–2031; this is a proposal, and no money is set aside yet ([Verkkouutiset](https://www.verkkouutiset.fi/a/syovan-hoidon-kulut-kasvamassa-800-miljoonalla-eurolla-naita-toimia-ehdotetaan/)).

### 6b. FICAN (Finnish Cancer Centres network)
- **What:** National coordinating unit plus five regional centres at university hospitals (South/HYKS, East, Mid/Tays, North, West/Turku). Regional centres apply national guidelines. ([FICAN](https://www.fican.fi/))
- **Start:** Year not verified (a 2019 claim could not be confirmed). [source needed]
- **Delay:** No evaluation found. FICAN has asked counties to report early-detection rates and time from suspected cancer referral to diagnosis. [source needed]
- **Confidence:** Medium.

### 6c. Omaolo symptom checker
- **What:** Online symptom checker for the public.
- **Start:** Project ran Jan 2016 to Aug 2018 (Ministry). [source needed] Name Omaolo came later. Ownership moved to SoteDigi Oy in 2018. [source needed] Kela's role not confirmed.
- **Quality:** [Liu et al. 2024, *JMIR Human Factors*, DOI 10.2196/55099](https://doi.org/10.2196/55099) (corrected: the "journal.fi, DOI not retrieved" study is this paper). Urgent cases (344 with a nurse-judged urgent triage): 70.9% exact match, 62.6% sensitivity, 69.2% specificity. The symptom list has no cancer symptoms. [source needed]
- **Delay / stage / SES:** No cancer-referral evaluation found.
- **Confidence:** Medium.

### 6d. Terveyskylä portal and Syöpälinja advice line
- **Terveyskylä:** Public health information portal, content written by health professionals. Owner not confirmed as THL. [source needed] Not evaluated for cancer. Do not confuse with Terveyskirjasto.fi (run by Duodecim).
- **Syöpälinja:** Free advice line from the Cancer Societies of Finland (phone, email, chat). Nurses give general information; they do not diagnose. [source needed] No user evaluation or usage data found.
- **Confidence:** Low.

### 6e. Finnish fast-track pilots
- None found in any county. The UK FDS and a Swedish SCAN clinic model were named but not researched here.

### 6f. Finnish inequity evidence (register studies)
- **[Zai et al. 2025, *BMJ Public Health*, DOI 10.1136/bmjph-2025-002829](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjph-2025-002829)** (checked; numbers confirmed in the full text on [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12551501/)). 469,522 diagnoses, 2000–2019. Stage IV odds ratio, low vs high education: men 1.10 (95% CI 1.08–1.12), women 1.15 (1.13–1.17). Cancer mortality hazard ratio: men 1.13 (1.10–1.16), women 1.14 (1.11–1.17). Income shows a similar pattern (no numbers in text). Region is only a control variable. Delay not measured. Stage was missing for about 30% of cases.
- **[Pokhrel et al. 2010, *British Journal of Cancer*, DOI 10.1038/sj.bjc.6605861](https://doi.org/10.1038/sj.bjc.6605861)** (checked; abstract confirmed on [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2965870)). Lower education had worse survival at almost all sites. Part of the gap is later-stage diagnosis.
- **[Seikkula et al. 2017, *International Journal of Cancer*, DOI 10.1002/ijc.31109](https://doi.org/10.1002/ijc.31109).** Prostate cancer: SES and stage/survival before and after PSA testing. Publisher page blocked; numbers not verified.
- **[Hermiö et al. 2026, *Acta Oncologica*, DOI 10.2340/1651-226X.2026.44788](https://doi.org/10.2340/1651-226x.2026.44788)** (checked). Care contacts in the year before colorectal cancer diagnosis. Findings not read.
- **Patient survey (Suomen Syöpäpotilaat ry):** 684 responses ([sttinfo](https://www.sttinfo.fi/tiedote/70972938/kyselyselvitys-joka-kolmas-syopapotilas-kokee-diagnoosin-viivastyneen?publisherId=69820264)). Over half felt symptoms were not taken seriously; about a third felt diagnosis was delayed. Not peer-reviewed; date not verified.
- **Confidence:** High for education gaps in stage and survival; none for delay.

## What the evidence says, by question

Our reading:

- **Did it cut delay?** Yes for some steps and some patients (Denmark CPP, PNRP Ohio trial, Norway lung). Often not for the full path (Norway treatment start, Sweden targets missed). See sections 1a, 2, 5b and 3.
- **Did it change stage or survival?** Rarely measured. Only UK 2WW (mixed) and US navigation (not randomised for stage) touch it.
- **Did it narrow SES or regional gaps?** Only PNRP shows a narrowed racial gap (not closed). Denmark, Norway, Sweden, UK: not tested or no split. Sweden shows vulnerable patients crowded out.
- **Why did it work or fail?** Named people or fast visits help people already inside the system. Pathways that are not linked to the rest of the care chain leave the slow steps unchanged. Missing data means nobody can see the gaps.

## Method note

Three research digests (Nordic; UK and US; Finland), each by a Haiku sub-agent using WebSearch, WebFetch and the reference-lookup tools. DOIs marked "checked" were resolved by metadata lookup (lookup_reference), which also returned the DOI link used here. Full-text verification was not done for most papers; some numbers come from abstracts or search summaries. Two papers were checked against their full text (Zai 2025, Pokhrel 2010 via PMC). Figures marked "snippet" or "not verified" should be read in the original before use. Source sweep (2026-11-14) added links and corrections; the note "(corrected)" marks changed claims.
