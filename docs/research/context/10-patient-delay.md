# Patient delay: what drives it (Finland first, then Nordic and EU)

Date: 2026-11-14. Research only. No product ideas.

Tags: **[opened]** = I read the page or PDF. **[secondary]** = a news or press summary of a report. **[snippet]** = only a search summary; not checked. **[source needed]** = no working URL found yet.

## Short answer

- Finland has no study that measures patient delay with fear, distrust, health literacy and navigation together. The nearest Finnish data is a 2024 panel survey on cancer risk knowledge, which asked about avoiding care ([Syöpäriskitietoisuusraportti 2024](https://www.syopajarjestot.fi/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Syopariskitietoisuusraportti_2024_2.pdf)).
- The strongest Finnish signal: cost and fear of results are the top reasons people give for putting off a check ([same survey](https://www.syopajarjestot.fi/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Syopariskitietoisuusraportti_2024_2.pdf)).
- Health literacy in Finland is lower with less education and lower income ([Prinkey et al. 2026](http://jyx.jyu.fi/jyx/Record/jyx_123456789_112993)). Age effects are in the [IJPH 2026 paper](https://www.ssph-journal.org/articles/10.3389/ijph.2026.1609337/full). Full national shares by education and age were not found.
- Interpreter rule: free interpretation when staff judge it needed ([eu-healthcare.fi](https://www.eu-healthcare.fi/?p=435814)). Use in practice is unmeasured in Finland.

## 1. Symptom awareness and avoiding care

**Finland: Syöpäriskitietoisuusraportti 2024 (Syöpäjärjestöt) [opened, PDF text]** ([PDF](https://www.syopajarjestot.fi/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Syopariskitietoisuusraportti_2024_2.pdf))
- Panel survey by Innolink, November–December 2024, aged 18–70. Sample size not rechecked in this pass.
- It measures knowledge of cancer risk factors and reported avoidance of care. It is not a test of symptom awareness.
- "Nearly a quarter" (lähes neljännes) of respondents had avoided contacting health care because of symptoms that could be cancer.
- Fear of seeking care was higher among younger respondents and in Southern Finland and Uusimaa. The gap between men and women was small.
- 44% found it hard to see a doctor (quite or very hard). 49% found it easy. Those who avoided care found access slightly harder.
- Among avoiders, reasons (often / sometimes): fear of findings 37% / 42%; cannot afford tests 33% / 48%; too busy 23% / 37%. These bases are only the people who avoided care.
- Limit: the report text I read has no education or income split of avoidance.

**Finland: men's survey, Syöpäsäätiö, n = 539 [secondary]**
- Only 1 in 4 men would contact health care at once if they noticed cancer symptoms. Main reasons for waiting: they thought the symptoms were not serious, or would go away. [source needed: survey release not identified in this pass.]
- Corrected: fieldwork year. One source says January 2026, another says the methodology slide gives 5–16 January 2025. The sources disagree; treat the year as unconfirmed.

**Denmark: Hvidberg et al. 2014, BMC Cancer, PMC4138385 [opened]** ([paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4138385/))
- Measure: ABC (Awareness and Beliefs about Cancer). Telephone survey, n = 3,000 (1,000 aged 30–49, 2,000 aged 50+), fieldwork 31 May to 4 July 2011. Response rate 36.7%.
- Low awareness = recognising fewer than 9 cancer symptoms (the paper's table label "<9 cancer symptoms"). Corrected: the earlier note said "of 11".
- Adjusted prevalence ratio, low vs high education: 1.57 (95% CI 1.39–1.78).
- Adjusted prevalence ratio, low vs high household income: 1.33 (95% CI 1.15–1.54). Not adjusted for education, because of overlap between the two.
- The abstract says: "A strong socio-economic gradient in cancer awareness was found."

**Six-country ABC comparison, age-risk awareness, age 50+ [snippet]**
- Share who knew cancer risk rises with age: Denmark 25%, Norway 29%, Sweden 38%. [source needed]
- Finland is not in the snippet's country list. No Finnish ABC or CAM data found.

**Norway: interval breast cancer, qualitative [snippet]**
- Women often saw non-lump symptoms as cancer only in hindsight.
- Trust in a normal screening result could lead women to delay. Author and year not captured. [source needed]

**Denmark: qualitative, 30 cancer patients [snippet]**
- Delay came from how patients read their symptoms, before they sought care. Author and year not captured. [source needed]

**International synthesis of help-seeking qualitative studies [snippet, PubMed ID 16139657]**
- Key themes in the search summary: recognising and interpreting symptoms, and fear of going to the doctor. The PubMed page did not load in this pass. [source needed]

Other points:
- Cost: OECD 2023 (EU-SILC based) says more than 1 in 10 Finns with a care need went without it in 2023 [snippet]. [source needed] Eurostat 2024: waiting lists were the most frequent reason for unmet need in Finland [snippet]. [source needed] So cost and waiting may both matter.
- Cost gradient in the EU, 2024: 2.1% of people in the lowest income quintile report unmet need due to cost, vs 1.4% in the highest [snippet]. [source needed] Finland-by-quintile figure not checked; Eurostat's income-quintile tables should have it.
- Helsinki-area head and neck cancer study: n = 142, median patient delay 35 days, 73% sought care within 3 months [snippet; source not identified]. [source needed]
- Atula et al. 2024, BMC Cancer, n = 203, oral and laryngeal cancer, doi 10.1186/s12885-024-12517-x ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12885-024-12517-x)): no link between patient delay and psychosocial scores. Larger tumours linked to lower SES [snippet; full text not opened, the publisher page needs a Springer login].
- Kuitunen 2026, Cancer Medicine, lymphoma: route (public vs private or occupational) mattered more than income or education [snippet, [research.utu.fi](https://research.utu.fi/converis/portal/detail/Publication/523655373)]. Team summary: [05 issues file](../../discovery/05-issues-and-causes.md), lymphoma row. Full text not read.

## 3. Health literacy (Finland)

**HLS19-Q12, Finland, general population [record opened; figures not rechecked in this pass]**
- Prinkey et al. 2026, *European Journal of Public Health* 36(4). Data 2022–23, Terve Suomi and MoniSuomi surveys. n = 9,717, aged 20–74. ([record](http://jyx.jyu.fi/jyx/Record/jyx_123456789_112993))
- Limited health literacy: **15%** of the general population (95% CI 13.2–15.9).
- Limited health literacy: **42%** of the migrant-origin population (95% CI 40.1–43.4).
- Lower education and income difficulties are linked with limited health literacy. The record gives no shares by education or income, and no age figures.

**HLS-Q12, Finland, 2024 survey [opened]**
- IJPH 2026, doi 10.3389/ijph.2026.1609337 ([article](https://www.ssph-journal.org/articles/10.3389/ijph.2026.1609337/full)). Analysed n = 7,077 (national n = 4,003).
- Gives only latent means, not shares. Not comparable with the 15% above.
- Education vs basic education (latent mean, all significant): vocational 0.19, general upper secondary 0.20, post-secondary 0.32, university of applied sciences 0.52, university 0.56.
- Age vs 18–34: 35–49 −0.06 (not significant), 50–64 −0.02 (not significant), 65–89 −0.12 (significant).

**Full HLS19 Finland share: not found.** Across 17 countries, limited health literacy ranges 25–72% [snippet]. [source needed] Country tables are at m-pohl.net; the PDF was too large to fetch.

**Digital skills, Finland, 2023 [secondary]**
- About half of people aged 65–74 have basic digital skills. About a fifth of people aged 75–89 do (corrected: one news summary gives 22%; the Gerontechnology abstract says "about a fifth"). Sources: [ArcticToday](https://www.arctictoday.com/finland-is-europes-most-digitalised-country-but-older-people-are-still-left-behind/) and [Gerontechnology](https://journal.gerontechnology.org/currentIssueContent.aspx?aid=4957). The Digibarometer 2023 report itself was not found.
- Finland's ombudsman for older people estimates 500,000–600,000 people over 65 lack digital skills [secondary]. [source needed]

## 4. Digital-first access as a barrier

- **No Finnish study found** on the share of people who cannot use digital booking, or on Omaolo chat use by age or education.
- **Omaolo symptom checker validation, Liu, Koskela and Kaila, JMIR Human Factors 2024, PMID 39326038 ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39326038/), [JMIR](https://humanfactors.jmir.org/2024/1/e55099)) [search summary, checked]:**
  - 877 patients at walk-in centres, checked on arrival, then triaged by a nurse.
  - Advice judged safe in 856 of 877 cases (97.6%).
  - Exact agreement with the nurse 53.7%. If one level more cautious counts, 66.6%.
  - No age or education split in the summary.
- **Access numbers, THL 2025 [secondary, Helsinki Times, article dated 3 June 2026]:** 59% of adults saw a doctor within two weeks in 2025. A 2025 amendment raised the adult primary-care limit from 14 days to three months. The 14-day rule still applies to people under 23. ([Helsinki Times](https://www.helsinkitimes.fi/lifestyle/health-a-wellbeing/28899-primary-care-waiting-times-increased-in-2025-says-thl.html))
- **Digital exclusion [snippet]:** A representative Finnish study (n = 4,495) found older adults at risk; education and income also contribute. Not opened. [source needed]
- **Phone access [snippet]:** A study of Finnish health centres (QUALICOPC, n = 1,196) names ease of phoning as important to patients. No phone-queue data. [source needed]
- **Gap:** Nothing found that measures phone or digital booking failure by age, education or language in Finland.

## 5. Language and interpreters

**Rule [opened, eu-healthcare.fi]** ([page](https://www.eu-healthcare.fi/?p=435814))
- "If the patient and the treatment personnel do not have a common language, interpretation will be arranged."
- Interpretation is free of charge to the patient when staff consider it necessary. Translation of written material is not covered.
- The page does not name a law or say how to book an interpreter.

**Law [secondary, unverified]**
- Helsinki city guideline says the right to interpreting applies when staff do not speak the patient's language. It cites Health Care Act 1326/2010 §6 with Patient Act §3 and Client Act §4. [source needed]
- Could not confirm §6 text. Check on Finlex before relying on it.

**Use in practice**
- Nepali migrants, Khanal 2025, *BMC Health Services Research* 25:577, published 22 April 2025 ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12013081/)) [opened]: 27 face-to-face interviews in Nepali, February–August 2023.
  - The article says anyone, including friends and family, can act as interpreter. Participants described using a daughter and a friend.
  - One participant said an operation was postponed. The article does not verify this.
  - One participant said medication instructions were all in Finnish. The article says information was "predominantly provided in the local language."
  - Limit: small sample; findings come from participants' own accounts.
- Turku study, eight public health nurses at one health centre [snippet]: interpreter use adds to nurses' work and can cause ethical problems. [source needed]
- No national interpreter-use data for Finnish primary care found.
- EU systematic review, "Language barriers for primary care access in Europe" (PMC9594485) [snippet]. Not opened; author name differs between search snippets. [source needed]

## Biggest gap

No Finnish study measures delay before first contact, with fear, cost, distrust, navigation and digital or phone access by education and age. The Finnish data above measures attitudes or avoidance, not time to first contact. The next data needs: (1) the full HLS19 Finland table by education and age, (2) Finnish time from symptom to first contact, and (3) phone and digital booking failure by age and language.
