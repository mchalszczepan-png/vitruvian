# 08 — Primary care "fast lanes" for suspected cancer in Finland

Date: 2026-11-14. Research only. No product ideas.

Tags used:
- **[read]** = I opened the page and read it.
- **[snippet]** = I only saw a search summary. Check the page before quoting.
- **[negative]** = I searched and found nothing. Not proof that nothing exists.

## Short answer

- No national or county fast track for suspected cancer from primary care was found. Yle MOT says Finland "has no fast-track system for suspected cancer" **[read]**, https://yle.fi/a/74-20251023.
- Some regions have urgent referral routes (for example lung cancer). I found no evaluation of any of them.
- The only Finnish outcome evidence is old: in a 1996 cohort, patients "overlooked" at the first visit died more often **[snippet, https://www.cmaj.ca/content/174/6/779]**.
- Biggest gap: no national data on how long it takes from first GP contact to a scan or specialist visit, by county.

## 1. Fast tracks from primary care

- Yle MOT: no fast track exists. Kuittinen (University of Eastern Finland) wants one, plus faster scans and better GP training **[read]** — https://yle.fi/a/74-20251023
- Searches for Varha, TAYS, KYS, OYS and HUS "syöpäepäily" fast-track units, or "nopean diagnostiikan yksikkö", found nothing **[negative]**.
- HUS head-and-neck referral guide (2020): if malignancy is suspected, the GP should refer at once. The tumour clinic then does the imaging. The guide says this often shortens delays **[snippet]** — https://production.hus-ref.prod.gc.hus.fi/sites/default/files/2020-10/Tuumoripotilaan%20l%C3%A4heteohje.pdf (fetch failed, so I only have the search summary).
- Pirkanmaa lung cancer pathway (development version): suspected lung cancer gets an urgent referral to the central hospital lung clinic. Referrals are usually handled within 1–2 working days **[snippet]** — https://pirha.fi/documents/d/kehitysversio/keuhkosyopapotilaan-hoitopolku
- Lung pathways in several regions start with the GP visit, exam and chest X-ray. An urgent referral follows if cancer is suspected **[snippet, source needed]**.
- A search summary also said that in Sweden's first diagnostic centre fewer than half of patients were investigated within 15 days. I have no URL for this **[source needed]**. Do not use it until checked.

Evaluation of Finnish fast-track units: **none found**.

## 2. GP direct orders for CT/MRI/endoscopy, and waits

- Rules differ by region. No national rule found **[negative]**.
- Oulu (Northern Ostrobothnia, PPSHP) rule: CT and MRI need a specialist referral, or the referral must say a specialist was consulted. Times are booked by phone **[snippet]** — https://www.ppshp.fi/dokumentit/Kuvantamisen%20ohje%20sislttyyppi/Oulun%20kaupungin%20terveysasemien%20kuvantamistutkimusten%20ajanvaraus%20oys%20kuv%20til.docx
- Same Oulu rules: for adults, a GP can order urgently only a leg-vein ultrasound. Urgent CT is mostly done through emergency X-ray **[snippet]**.
- Remote referrals ("etälähete") for ultrasound, CT or MRI are allowed under conditions **[snippet, source needed]**.
- Wait times by county: **none found for CT/MRI**.
- Helsinki / MSD study (Khalife et al., Acta Oncologica 2025, DOI 10.2340/1651-226X.2025.42778, https://medicaljournalssweden.se/actaoncologica/article/view/42778). The "42 lung cancer specialists surveyed 2023–24" figure is not confirmed **[source needed]**. **[read]**:
  - Finland has the lowest primary-care access to CT and the longest primary-care waits among the Nordic countries **[snippet, Acta Oncologica summary, https://medicaljournalssweden.se/actaoncologica/article/view/42778]**.
  - Average wait for a primary-care appointment: about 2 weeks in Finland, usually under 1 week elsewhere in the Nordics.
  - In Finland, 40–60% of patients see a nurse at the first visit. Elsewhere they usually see a doctor.
  - Source: https://www.helsinki.fi/fi/uutiset/terveys/miksi-suomessa-keuhkosyopaan-kuollaan-aiemmin-kuin-pohjoismaissa
- Yle MOT lung cancer data (unpublished study by Kuittinen): symptom to treatment about 155 days. Split: over 50 days patient, 41 days primary care, 58 days specialist care. Sweden 107 days, Denmark 67 days **[read]** — https://yle.fi/a/74-20251023

## 3. Referral rejection/return and e-consultation

- Referral returns or rejections for cancer referrals (lähetteen palautus / hylkäys): **no Finnish rate found** **[negative]**.
- HUS guide says the referral needs a clear summary. It warns not to use an ICD-10 C (cancer) code unless cancer is confirmed by histology **[snippet]**. Incomplete or wrong coding is a likely reason for returns. This is my inference, not a sourced number.
- Lääkärilehti 2025 editorial: a new guidance lists what a referral document must contain **[snippet, URL not kept, source needed]**. This is a lead to read.
- THL survey of municipal consultation arrangements: access to specialist consultation varies a lot between health centres. The survey says consultation can prevent unplanned specialist referrals **[snippet]** — https://www.verkkouutiset.fi/a/erikoislaakarikonsultaatioiden-saatavuus-vaihtelee-terveyskeskuksissa-59786/ . It does not mention cancer.
- e-consultation (sähköinen konsultaatio) in Finnish cancer care: **none found**.
- International e-consult evidence (mixed, not Finnish) **[snippet]**:
  - Los Angeles County: specialist waits fell about 17% after 3 years. A quarter of e-consults led to no referral. https://www.chcf.org/resource/examining-effects-electronic-consults-primary-care-providers/
  - English platform (BMJ Open): about 38% of e-consults ended in an in-person visit. Little immediate saving. [snippet, secondary summary: https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/digital-health-platforms-may-not-be-a-cure-all-solution; original BMJ Open paper not found]

## 4. National guidance: suspected cancer in primary care

- **Käypä hoito / Duodecim suspected-cancer page: NOT checked.** kaypahoito.fi was not opened. This is the main gap in this section.
- Duodecim lung update (2020): chest X-ray shadow, maybe with enlarged lymph nodes, plus symptoms → refer to lung clinic **[snippet, source needed]**.
- Duodecim review (2016): chest CT is the basic test for lung cancer extent **[snippet, source needed]**.
- Time limit: diagnosis and treatment must be arranged within 6 weeks of the referral arriving. This comes from the 2010 STM working group report (corrected: earlier note said a cancer-society working group; the search found no cancer-society source for it) **[snippet]** — https://www.iccp-portal.org/system/files/plans/selv_2010_6_syovan_hoito_verkko.pdf
- Symptoms that should always be checked for cancer (MTV article) **[read]**: unexplained weight loss; long unexplained pain; new hard lump; blood in stool or urine; persistent constipation. The article also lists warning signs for breast, brain, lung and bowel cancer. This is a press summary, not a formal list. https://www.mtvuutiset.fi/artikkeli/syopadiagnooseissa-on-viiveita-tiettyjen-oireiden-kohdalla-on-poissuljettava-syovan-mahdollisuus/9345888
- A national red-flag list like UK NICE NG12 **was not found for Finland** **[negative]**. NG12 exists for the UK. For some symptoms it asks for an urgent chest X-ray within 2 weeks **[snippet]** — https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng12/chapter/Recommendations
- Strategy context **[read/snippet]**:
  - National cancer strategy 2026–2035 ("Yhdessä syöpää vastaan") adopted Nov 2025 (press release, https://valtioneuvosto.fi/-/1271139/yhteinen-suunta-ihmislahtoiseen-ja-vaikuttavaan-syovan-ehkaisyyn-ja-hoitoon-kansallinen-syopastrategia-on-julkaistu). First goal: less cancer burden through prevention and early detection. [source needed for "first goal" wording; the press release says the strategy aims to reduce cancer burden through prevention and early detection]
  - Implementation is slow. The roadmap needs about €140 million **[source needed]**. (File 07 has FICAN's €139M funding ask for 2027–2031.) Next year's state budget has no money for better cancer outcomes (Yle MOT, https://yle.fi/a/74-20251023).
  - National lung cancer programme 2026–2035 started May 2026, aiming to equalise diagnosis and treatment **[snippet]** — https://www.filha.fi/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Valtakunnallinen-keuhkosyopaohjelma-2026-2035_180526_preprint.pdf
- Treatment guarantee moved from 2 weeks to 3 months. Kuittinen says this can send wrongly "non-urgent" patients to long waits **[read]**. Sources for the rule changes: 2-week step in Sept 2023 (https://valtioneuvosto.fi/-/1271139/perusterveydenhuollon-hoitotakuu-tiukkenee-kahteen-viikkoon-syyskuussa-2023-); 1.1.2025 rules, 3 months for adults 23+ and 14 days under 23 (https://valtioneuvosto.fi/-/1271139/miten-hoitotakuuta-koskevia-saannoksia-sovelletaan-stm-julkaisi-ohjeet-hyvinvointialueille).

## 5. Continuity with the same GP, and cancer delay

Finland:
- Kuitunen et al., Cancer Medicine 2026: 160 patients with diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, 7 hospitals, 2014–2020. Age, income and job decide which provider a patient first contacts. Delays differ by provider **[snippet]** — https://research.utu.fi/converis/portal/detail/Publication/523655373
- Related Finnish lymphoma analysis (Tokola et al., Cancer Medicine 2025, same 160-patient DLBCL cohort; corrected: the first author is Tokola, not Kuitunen; URL **[source needed]**) **[snippet]**: mean symptom-to-treatment 146 days; patient delay before first contact 54 days; symptom-to-biopsy 130 days.
  - Derived by me: 146 − 54 = 92 days, about 63% of the total, came after first contact.
  - This matches the claim that most delay is after first contact. The snippet does not split that 92 days between GP and hospital.
- MOT lung data (see section 2): primary care 41 days of 155 **[read]**.
- Patient survey (Suomen Syöpäpotilaat ry, press release 12 Mar 2025) **[read]**:
  - 684 responses. Fieldwork around the turn of 2024–25. Questionnaire "Diagnoosi, syöpähoito ja seuranta". Method of delivery not stated.
  - About 30% felt the diagnosis was delayed.
  - Over half felt symptoms were not taken seriously, the cause was not found, or a non-cancer cause was suspected.
  - One third had at least 3 contacts with healthcare before the diagnosis was confirmed.
  - Over half saw a doctor within 3 months of first noticing symptoms.
  - Self-selected patient group, not a random sample. https://www.sttinfo.fi/tiedote/70972938/kyselyselvitys-joka-kolmas-syopapotilas-kokee-diagnoosin-viivastyneen?publisherId=69820264
- Potilasvakuutuskeskus (compensation data) **[snippet, https://www.laakarilehti.fi/tieteessa/alkuperaistutkimukset/diagnoosiviiveet-ovat-syyna-viidesosaan-korvattavista-potilasvahingoista/en]**: 326 compensated cancer diagnosis delays, about 50 per year. "Over six years" is our inference from three 2-year periods; the summary does not say it. Largest groups: breast, brain, lung, colorectal. Only cases reported by patients or families count, so this undercounts.

Nordic and other:
- Koskela et al., Scand J Prim Health Care 2024 (Tampere): qualitative online survey of 159 GPs in 23 European countries about delayed cancer diagnoses. Authors say faster diagnosis needs a long-term, holistic approach with follow-up and reassessment **[snippet]** — https://boris.unibe.ch/190620/8/Koskela_ScandJPrimHealthCare_2024.pdf
- Denmark (national registry, about 150,216 cancer patients vs 1.5 million controls, 2,665 practices): GP switching differs by cancer type. Authors say this points to diagnostic pathway problems for some cancers. Study does not prove delay **[snippet]** — https://informahealthcare.com/doi/ref/10.1080/02813432.2023.2296117 (author and year not checked).
- Denmark: longer travel distance to the GP was linked to shorter patient and primary-care intervals. Distance was not simply a barrier **[snippet, source needed]**. This works against a simple "same GP = faster" story.
- Denmark (pancreatic cancer): if the GP did not suspect cancer, the diagnostic interval was longer **[snippet, source needed]**.
- Norway (Sandvik et al., BJGP): continuity linked to mortality, hospital use and out-of-hours use. No cancer-delay result found **[snippet, source needed]**.
- Finland: **no study found linking same-GP continuity to cancer delay** **[negative]**.

## Evidence of effect (summary)

| Idea | Finnish evidence | Effect found |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cancer fast track from primary care | None found | Unknown |
| Overlooked at first visit | 1996 head-and-neck cohort: 221 patients, 20% (45) had no referral and no follow-up at first visit. Authors: Oulu and Kuopio, CMAJ 2006 **[snippet]** — https://www.cmaj.ca/content/174/6/779 | Death risk at 3 years higher (adjusted HR 1.89, 95% CI 1.03–3.45) |

| Direct CT/MRI from GP | Regional rules only | No wait data by county |

Note: the 1996 study used old data. Health centres and imaging have changed since then.

## Biggest gaps (ranked)

1. **No Finnish wait data** from first GP contact to CT/MRI/endoscopy or specialist visit, by county. MOT says Finland has no national cancer quality registry. Denmark's registry helped lymphoma and lung outcomes **[read]** (https://yle.fi/a/74-20251023).
2. **No evaluation** of any Finnish urgent or fast route from primary care.
3. **No published referral return or rejection rate** for cancer referrals.
4. **Käypä hoito text not read.** Needs a direct check of kaypahoito.fi.
5. **Lääkärilehti and Terveysportti not searched** (paywalled or not indexed). Lead: the 2025 Lääkärilehti editorial on referral content.
