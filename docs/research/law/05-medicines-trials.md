# 05 · Medicines and trials: rules that delay cancer treatment or make it unequal

Scope: how new cancer medicines reach Finnish patients (hospital and outpatient), the EU HTA Regulation, and clinical trial access. Date of research: 2026-11-13.

Tool note: context.dev search was rate-limited (shared across six researchers). Two context.dev searches succeeded before the limit. The rest fell back to WebSearch and WebFetch, per the team instruction. EUR-Lex is unreachable from this environment (TLS certificate mismatch in both WebFetch and curl), so EU regulation article numbers are secondary-source only (marked medium). PDFs were downloaded with curl and read with pdftotext.

Terms used: **Palko** = Terveydenhuollon palveluvalikoimaneuvosto, the national service-selection council. Its English name is **COHERE Finland**. **Hila** = Lääkkeiden hintalautakunta, the Finnish Pharmaceutical Pricing Board, which decides Kela reimbursement and the maximum reimbursement price (corrected from "wholesale price"; source: Hila FAQ https://www.hila.fi/toiminta-ja-organisaatio/usein-kysytyt-kysymykset/). **W.A.I.T.** = EFPIA "Patients W.A.I.T." indicator, the time from EU marketing authorisation to patient availability. **JCA** = EU joint clinical assessment. **CTR** = EU Clinical Trials Regulation. **FinCCHTA** = Finnish Coordinating Center for Health Technology Assessment. **Hyvinvointialue** = wellbeing services county.

## 2. Inefficiencies in the law and process

Confidence: high = primary source read; medium = secondary source or search excerpt; low = inference.

### A. Hospital medicines (new cancer drugs in hospital wards)

**A1. No single national decision. Each wellbeing area decides adoption.**
- Rule: the final adoption decision is made by each wellbeing area (STM 2026, p. 8). Palko recommendations guide but do not bind. (Binding status of Palko advice per HE 90/2026 is UNVERIFIED; see A2.)
- Why it hurts: the same drug can be in use in one county and not in another. Patients' time to treatment depends on where they live.
- Stage: hospital adoption (after reimbursement or in parallel).
- Evidence: the strategy says there is currently **no uniform application procedure and no uniform criteria** for deciding which process a new hospital medicine goes through (strategy p. 52). It also says the decentralised model "causes many challenges", and that case-by-case patient decisions are made in each area on top of the systematic review.
- Source URLs: https://files.hackjunction.app/resources/Kansallinen_syopastrategia_2026_2035_FINAL.pdf (p. 52) ; STM 2026 p. 8.
- Confidence: **high**.

**A2. Palko recommendations are guiding, not binding, and cover only part of the service range.**
- Rule: Palko gives national recommendations instead of binding decisions (HE 90/2026, committee change; https://www.edilex.fi/he/20260090); STM 2026 p. 14 calls them guidance. Wellbeing areas have committed to follow them [source needed]. The recommendations cover only a limited part of the service range (HE 90/2026, via search summary).
- Why it hurts: where there is no recommendation, the county or hospital decides alone, so adoption depends on local capacity.
- Evidence: UNVERIFIED: a search excerpt reportedly says a Keski-Suomi area does not use a drug after a negative Palko finding. Source not confirmed. HE 90/2026 says recent recommendations for drugs in the service range have all been **conditional** [source needed] (search summary only).
- Source: https://www.edilex.fi/he/20260090 (not opened; search summary only) ; https://palveluvalikoima.fi/valmiit-suositukset
- Confidence: **medium**. Final binding status UNVERIFIED (bill text not read).

**A3. Regional variation in uptake of outpatient cancer medicines (evidence).**
- Rule: wellbeing areas, not a national body, choose which medicines to adopt and the timing.
- Evidence: Dosis 2024 study (Huttu, Klintrup, Kurko, Koskinen) of 43 outpatient cancer drugs reimbursed after 2010–2021, using Kela data by special responsibility area. HYKS adopted **39 of 43 (91%)**, TAYS 39, OYS 37 (86%), TYKS 37, and KYS **35 of 43 (81%)** (body table of the full issue; corrected: the abstract names HYKS and TYKS for 39). Most drugs were taken up within a month of reimbursement. The split of 79% adopted in all areas, 14% in some, 7% in none is confirmed in the full text.
- Source: https://dosis.fi/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/240_Dosis_324_Huttu.pdf (article, search excerpt) ; full issue https://dosis.fi/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Dosis_324.pdf (checked)
- Confidence: **high** for the 39 / 37 / 35 counts and the 79 / 14 / 7 split (full issue checked). The note that in Finland, unlike the other Nordic countries, wellbeing areas determine the sample, which makes comparison hard, is from the search excerpt only [source needed].

**A4. Price negotiation is county-level and after Palko.**
- Rule: the wellbeing area buys drugs and negotiates price itself. Negotiation happens only after Palko's recommendation [source needed].
- Why it hurts: the price step adds time after the clinical recommendation, and each county negotiates separately.
- Source: strategy p. 52 (calls for a national price-negotiation mechanism and conditional adoption; it does not say negotiation should move earlier; corrected).
- Confidence: **medium**.

**A5. Dual-channel financing splits incentives.**
- Rule: outpatient drugs are paid by Kela, but drugs given in hospitals or outpatient clinics are paid by the wellbeing area. STM 2026 p. 27 says this dual channel raises the risk that medicine choices do not consider treatment outcomes.
- Why it hurts: a drug can be cheaper for the county to avoid than for Kela to pay. In July 2026 Kela stopped paying some expensive drugs given in public care, moving costs to the counties. The ministry says some counties used this as a saving measure. The counties dispute that (Yle, 14.7.2026, updated 27.7.2026).
- Stage: outpatient → administered-in-clinic boundary (treatment start can be delayed by billing).
- Source: https://yle.fi/a/74-20236301?origin=rss ; STM 2026 p. 27 (risk) and p. 28 (incentive and cost-shift wording; corrected from p. 34).
- Confidence: **high** for the Yle facts; **medium** for the effect on patients (the Yle article says patients should still receive drugs).

### B. Outpatient medicines (Kela reimbursement via Hila)

**B1. Statutory 180-day limit for Hila decisions, but real timelines are long and have clock stops.**
- Rule: the 180-day maximum comes from the EU Directive (secondary; corrected: the Hila FAQ does not state it). The 90-day price-increase figure [source needed]. Clock stops for information requests lengthen real timelines (EU source).
- Measured: 2024 median for basic reimbursement of new active substances was **100 days** if accepted and **155 days** if rejected. For special reimbursement, 96 days if accepted and 143 days if rejected.
- Why it hurts: the decision alone adds about 3–5 months before a drug can be prescribed in outpatient care.
- Source: https://www.hila.fi/toiminta-ja-organisaatio/tilastot/2024-2/ (verified, fetched) ; https://www.hila.fi/toiminta-ja-organisaatio/usein-kysytyt-kysymykset/ ; clock-stop effect: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12478080 (secondary).
- Confidence: **high** for the statutory and median figures (Hila); **medium** for clock-stop effect (EU secondary).

**B2. Sequencing rule: special reimbursement only after basic reimbursement.**
- Rule: for new active substances, Hila always handles the basic reimbursement application first. Special reimbursement can be applied for only after basic reimbursement is confirmed (Hila FAQ).
- Why it hurts: for expensive cancer drugs that need special (high-cost) reimbursement, the second decision adds time after the first.
- Source: https://www.hila.fi/toiminta-ja-organisaatio/usein-kysytyt-kysymykset/ (verified).
- Confidence: **high** (rule), **medium** (size of delay; 2024 median 96 days for accepted special applications, above).

**B3. Reimbursement has a fixed term that must be renewed.**
- Rule: "Päätös on voimassa määräajan, ja korvattavuus päättyy, ellei lääkeyritys hae sen uudistamista." A decision is valid for a set period. Reimbursement ends unless the company applies for renewal (Hila FAQ).
- Why it hurts: a lapsed decision can interrupt outpatient access. Renewal is a process risk, not a patient-level one.
- Source: https://www.hila.fi/toiminta-ja-organisaatio/usein-kysytyt-kysymykset/ (verified).
- Confidence: **high** (rule); **low** on how often this happens (no data found).

**B4. EU average time to availability is long, and oncology is slower in mean time.**
- Rule: W.A.I.T. measures time from marketing authorisation to availability (not reimbursement). Availability is measured against a public reimbursement list, which for most countries is the reimbursement list including limited availability (EFPIA 2024 methodology).
- Evidence (EFPIA W.A.I.T. 2025 survey, downloaded and parsed):
  - EU average time to availability, all products: **597 days** (2024: 578 days).
  - Oncology: average time **655 days**. Oncology availability rate **51%**. All-product availability rate **45%**.
  - Country spread: "gaps of more than two years between markets".
  - Finland's own figure: **UNVERIFIED**. The bar charts did not extract cleanly.
- Source: https://www.efpia.eu/media/mnfdwzax/efpia-patients-wait-indicator-2025.pdf (downloaded; EU figures verified).
- Confidence: **high** for EU averages; **UNVERIFIED** for Finland.

**B5. Finland's own reimbursement time is not published as one indicator.**
- The EFPIA 2023 survey (published 2024; corrected from "EFPIA 2024") mean EMA-to-reimbursement time ranged from under 100 days (Germany) to over 900 days (Lithuania) (secondary, PMC12478080). Finland's place: UNVERIFIED.
- Confidence: **low** for Finland.

### C. EU HTA Regulation (JCA)

**C1. JCA for oncology is live, but national use is undefined.**
- Rule: from 12 Jan 2025, JCA applies to new oncology medicines and ATMPs (L10). Member States keep economic evaluation, valuation, recommendations and decision-making (Fimea page, 11.12.2024; corrected from "non-clinical and pricing steps").
- Why it hurts: the JCA covers clinical comparison only. Finland's Hila pricing and county adoption steps still run separately, so JCA could add a parallel track rather than remove one.
- Source: Fimea page (above); Commission page (above).
- Confidence: **medium-high**.

**C2. Finland has no verified national law on how JCA feeds into Hila or Palko decisions.**
- Evidence: Fimea says further specification will come "if necessary" (Dec 2024). I found no Finnish implementing law (search, 2026). Fimea and Hila staff said key rules were still pending (search summary).
- Confidence: **medium** (absence of evidence). Status: UNVERIFIED.

### D. Clinical trials

**D1. Trial sites are small and not coordinated.**
- Rule: trials run hospital by hospital. Each hospital's patient numbers are small, and research cooperation is limited (strategy p. 46).
- Why it hurts: patients in regions without an active trial unit have fewer trial options. One third of clinical drug trials in Finland are cancer trials, and over half of early-phase trials (strategy p. 46; corrected from "a quarter"). Our reading: this concentrates trials in a few centres.
- Source: strategy p. 46 (https://files.hackjunction.app/resources/Kansallinen_syopastrategia_2026_2035_FINAL.pdf). 
- Confidence: **high** (statement); **UNVERIFIED** for regional trial-access numbers (none found).

**D2. No national feasibility service for industry and academic trials.**
- Evidence: the strategy's target state says feasibility data should be available nationally, so that enquiries can be answered quickly (p. 46). That implies it is not available now (inference).
- Confidence: **low-medium** (inference from target state).

**D3. Multinational vs national trial routes are not aligned.**
- Rule: the accelerated 30-day route (L9) applies to **Finland-only** applications. Multinational trials follow the EU CTR timelines (45 + up to 31 days in Part I, L8).
- Why it hurts: Finland-only trials can be faster than multinational ones, which may push international trial sites elsewhere.
- Source: https://fimea.fi/en/-/fimea-and-tukija-to-speed-up-evaluation-of-national-clinical-trials ; L8 (secondary).
- Confidence: **high** (Finland-only scope); **medium** (CTR days).

**D4. Trial recruitment target exists, but no published count by county.**
- Target: over 10% of cancer patients enrolled in intervention trials (strategy p. 5). No county-level data found. (Strategy p. 5 footnote: about 200 new clinical cancer trials per year by 2035 (earlier UNVERIFIED flag removed; https://files.hackjunction.app/resources/Kansallinen_syopastrategia_2026_2035_FINAL.pdf).)
- Confidence: **high** (target); **UNVERIFIED** (current baseline).

## 3. Ways to work with the current law (what a county, cooperation area or AI tool could do now)

Each item lists the legal or procedural basis. Items marked UNVERIFIED need a check before use.

1. **Counties publish a dated adoption tracker for new cancer medicines.**
   - What: each county sets a public decision date after each Palko recommendation (or after a FinCCHTA hospital assessment for drugs without one).
   - Basis: each wellbeing area makes the final adoption decision (STM 2026 p. 8). Palko recommends (same page). FinCCHTA covers drugs without a recommendation (p. 14).
   - Impact: directly addresses the "no uniform procedure" gap (strategy p. 52).

2. **Use the public Hila pipeline to plan adoption before the decision.**
   - What: Hila publishes a monthly list of received applications (Hila FAQ). A county can track which cancer drugs are in the pipeline and prepare local adoption and pricing work before the Hila decision.
   - Basis: Hila FAQ says it publishes a monthly list of received applications (https://www.hila.fi/toiminta-ja-organisaatio/usein-kysytyt-kysymykset/). Pre-work is an internal county task, no law needed.
   - Impact: attacks the sequential delay (Hila 100–155 days, then county adoption).

3. **Forecast Hila decision dates with the published medians.**
   - What: for each application, use the 2024 medians (100 days accepted, 155 days rejected; special reimbursement 96/143 days) to give a planning window.
   - Basis: Hila 2024 statistics page (verified; https://www.hila.fi/toiminta-ja-organisaatio/tilastot/2024-2/).
   - Note: medians are not a legal promise. The 180-day limit is the maximum (EU Directive, see L2).

4. **Run the Dosis-style uptake analysis every year, from Kela data.**
   - What: count how many outpatient cancer drugs each special responsibility area has adopted, and how fast (time from first reimbursement to first purchase). This is the method used in the Dosis 2024 study.
   - Basis: Kela statistics are public [source needed] (the Dosis study used them: https://dosis.fi/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Dosis_324.pdf). Method is replicable without patient data.
   - Impact: produces the regional gap evidence that the brief asks for. An AI tool can refresh the number each quarter.
   - Caveat: the exact Kela data access route is UNVERIFIED.

5. **Use the Kela 2026 rule to prevent cost-shift delays.**
   - What: counties document where, by whom and under which organisation a drug is given (Kela now asks for this, Yle 2026: https://yle.fi/a/74-20236301?origin=rss). A standard template avoids a refused claim that would delay treatment.
   - Basis: Kela's current application requirement (Yle, 2026). Practical, not new law.
   - Confidence: medium (article only; the Kela form is UNVERIFIED).

6. **Cooperation areas use their own medicine councils to speed adoption.**
   - What: a cooperation area (yhteistyöalue) could share one adoption decision and one price negotiation for its counties.
   - Basis: UNVERIFIED. Cooperation-area medicine councils are not confirmed in the STM 2026 text I read. The earlier citation to a "Ruskoaho report" and STM p. 34–35 was not checked. Joint procurement rules: UNVERIFIED (statute not checked).
   - Impact: fewer parallel county decisions; one shared negotiation.

7. **Use the 30-day national trial route to open trial sites faster.**
   - What: for Finland-only trials, submit through CTIS and plan for the 30-day decision. Counties with trial units can open sites quickly and list them.
   - Basis: Fimea and Tukija accelerated route, from 11 Feb 2026 (Fimea page: https://fimea.fi/en/-/fimea-and-tukija-to-speed-up-evaluation-of-national-clinical-trials).
   - Impact: more trial places for patients in counties without an active trial unit.

8. **Build a national trial feasibility list from existing hospital data.**
   - What: each cancer hospital lists open trials and eligibility criteria, updated monthly. A county or AI tool matches patients' regions to nearby open trials.
   - Basis: strategy target state (p. 46) asks for national feasibility data. No patient-level data needed (public aggregate only, per brief).
   - Caveat: the sharing agreements between hospitals are UNVERIFIED.

9. **Prepare JCA-ready clinical comparison for Fimea.**
   - What: counties and hospitals contribute to the Fimea/FinCCHTA process by listing the local standard of care for each oncology indication. That helps the JCA comparator and avoids local mismatch later.
   - Basis: Fimea participates in JCA scope definition for hospital medicines (Fimea page, Dec 2024: https://fimea.fi/en/-/eu-hta-regulation-to-be-applied-to-fimea-s-assessment-of-hospital-only-medicinal-products).
   - Confidence: medium (process not yet defined; mark UNVERIFIED for who submits).

10. **AI tool: adoption-gap monitor.**
    - What: combine (a) Palko recommendation list (palveluvalikoima.fi), (b) county adoption dates (from trackers in item 1), (c) Hila decision dates, and (d) Kela uptake data. Output: for each new cancer drug, the days from Hila decision to county adoption, by county.
    - Basis: all inputs are public aggregate data (brief). No patient-level data.
    - Impact: the gap is measurable and linked to the pathway stage (hospital adoption vs reimbursement).
