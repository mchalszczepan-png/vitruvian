# 02 — How Finnish health-care law organises county care, and what it means for cancer care

Researcher: legal track (Junction 2026, AstraZeneca track). Date: 2026-11-13.
Scope: organisation law, centralisation decree, funding law, FICAN, cross-county flows.

**Source tools.** Finlex text was read with context.dev `web-scrape` (highlights mode, query-targeted). That tool hit a 429 rate limit early; I used WebFetch and WebSearch as fallback for FICAN, the STM hoitotakuu release and the FICAN site. Some Finlex URLs returned navigation only; those are marked.

**Abbreviations.** HVA = hyvinvointialue (wellbeing services county). YTA = yhteistyöalue (cooperation area). YSA = erityisvastuualue. VN = Valtioneuvosto (Government). "Confidence" = how sure I am the claim matches the cited text (high = quoted from the law text; med = inferred from law text; low = my inference, no evidence found).

Helsinki note: the Finlex excerpts I read show that HUS maintains the Uusimaa university hospital (§34). There are 21 wellbeing services counties. The City of Helsinki is not one of them and organises its own health and social services ([VM, counties and collaborative areas](https://vm.fi/en/wellbeing-services-counties-and-municipalities); [Soteuudistus](https://soteuudistus.fi/en/frontpage)). The count was unverified in the first draft; it is now sourced.

FICAN note: FICAN's own site says it "on perustettu keskittämisasetuksen ohjaamana" (set up, guided by the centralisation decree), and it has five regional cancer centres plus a national coordination unit (source: https://www.fican.fi/, fetched via WebFetch). FICAN is not named in the 582/2017 excerpts I read. So no statute I found creates FICAN. Legal form, funding and governance: UNVERIFIED.

## 2. Inefficiencies in the law that hurt cancer care

Each row: the rule, why it hurts, pathway stage, evidence, confidence.

| # | Rule (source) | Why it hurts cancer care / regional inequality | Stage | Evidence | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2.1 | Access clocks in the Health Care Act are general, not cancer-specific. Specialised care: 3 weeks to assess the referral, 3 months if specialist review or imaging is needed, 6 months to treat after the need is found (1326/2010 §52). | A 6-month legal ceiling for specialised treatment is very long for cancer. The law does not set a shorter cancer clock, so the statute itself does not protect diagnosis-to-treatment time. Cancer-specific targets, if any, live in guidelines (UNVERIFIED, see §4). | Referral → diagnosis → treatment start | https://www.finlex.fi/fi/lainsaadanto/2010/1326 ; STM release (hoitotakuu, specialised care unchanged): https://valtioneuvosto.fi/-/1271139/perusterveydenhuollon-hoitotakuu-tiukkenee-kahteen-viikkoon-syyskuussa-2023- | High (law text); medium (effect on cancer timing needs data) |
| 2.2 | Hoitotakuu is tightened for primary care (planned 7 days from 1.11.2024, **reversed**; adults now 3 months, see 07-verification.md) but specialised care timing is unchanged (STM release). | Pressure moves to the primary-to-specialist interface. Referral is the gap the brief names. Many cancer patients enter via primary care ([source needed]), so a tighter primary clock alone does not shorten the specialist wait. | Referral | Same STM release | High (text); medium (inference about effect) |
| 2.3 | Each county organises its own services; cross-county care depends on a cooperation agreement that is renewed every council term (612 §36, §35). | Patients who need care in a neighbouring county depend on an agreement that may lag or differ by area. Our reading: rules and pathways may vary across the five cooperation areas ([source needed]). A new agreement every four years can mean changed referral rules mid-treatment. | Referral; treatment start | 612 §35, §36 (see §1 table) | High (text); low (effect on cancer delays — no source found) |
| 2.4 | Home county pays the treating county "without undue delay" for actual net costs (612 §57). Late payment interest only from 30 days after the invoice (612 §61). Detail goes to a STM decree (not fetched). | A cross-county treatment episode carries a billing step. The statute gives no fixed payment deadline, only "without undue delay" and a 30-day floor before interest. A possible effect is that receiving hospitals are less willing to take out-of-county patients (inference, no source). | Treatment start (out-of-county) | 612 §57, §61 text. Billing delays: no evidence found (search for "viivästys" found only partial hits; PYSY023 and PYSY028 named as instructions, not fetched). | High (rule); low (delay effect) |
| 2.5 | Centralisation floors (582 §5–7): cancer surgeries bundled to fewer hospitals; volume floors (breast ~150, rectum 50, colon/kidney/prostate ~70 per year). Floors can be waived by cooperation agreement. | Bundling cuts travel for the minority of patients who go to central hospitals, but patients far from the five hospitals face longer trips. Inequality comes from geography, not from the floor itself. Waivers are allowed case by case, so the rule is not uniform across areas. | Treatment start | 582/2017 §5–7 text (https://www.finlex.fi/fi/lainsaadanto/2017/582) | High (rule); medium (travel effect — no regional data fetched) |
| 2.6 | Deficit cover within 2 years (611 §115); temporary extension to 2027–2029 on application (611 §115a, in force 1.6.2026–31.12.2030). | Counties in deficit have a legal reason to cut or delay. Cancer staffing, radiotherapy and imaging are large, shared costs ([source needed]). Our reading: pressure may fall unevenly on counties with weaker tax bases. | Diagnosis (imaging); treatment start (staffing) | 611 §115, §115a text | Medium (law text; effect on cancer capacity: no source found) |
| 2.7 | Borrowing cap (611 §15): based on debt-service cover; computed from the budget of the previous year. | A county with weak cover cannot borrow for new cancer equipment (linear accelerators, scanners) even if it needs it. | Treatment start (capacity) | 611 §15 text | Medium (law text; effect: low, no source) |
| 2.8 | Funding formula is needs-based, but extra money for tasks like cancer is discretionary (617 §11, §26; [source needed]: the §11 and §26 text could not be fetched). No cancer-specific allocation found in the sections read. | Costs of expensive cancer treatment (drugs, radiotherapy, surgery volume) are not separately protected. Counties carry the cancer cost inside general budgets. | All stages | 617 §11, §26 text | Medium (text); low (absence of a cancer allocation — UNVERIFIED, I did not read all of 617) |
| 2.9 | National cancer planning is given to HUS by decree (582 §3), not to FICAN. FICAN is set up "guided by" the decree (fican.fi), with no statutory mandate found. | No legally clear owner of national cancer data, targets or follow-up. Coordination depends on goodwill and funding not set in statute. The national strategy's implementation (2026–2031 roadmap) runs through FICAN, per its site. | All stages (governance) | 582/2017 §3, §4; https://www.fican.fi/ (WebFetch) | High (decree text); medium (FICAN role) |

## 3. Ways to work WITH the current law (no law change needed)

| # | What a county, a cooperation area or an AI tool can do now | Legal basis | Stage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3.1 | Write cancer pathway indicators into the cooperation agreement for the next council term: referral-to-assessment days, assessment-to-treatment days, share of out-of-county treatment. Make these a standing item. | 612 §36: agreement must secure "yhdenvertainen saatavuus" and working care chains; it may cover any matter needed for those aims. https://www.finlex.fi/fi/lainsaadanto/2021/612 | Referral; treatment start |
| 3.2 | Agree a fixed invoice timetable and template for cross-county cancer care in the cooperation agreement, so the "without undue delay" rule (612 §57) has a clear meaning. The cooperation agreement may set cost-sharing terms (612 §36). | 612 §36 (cost-sharing for agreed responsibilities) and §57 ("unless the agreement says otherwise"). | Treatment start |
| 3.3 | Use the volume-floor exception on purpose: where a local central hospital is justified (on-call surgery, staffing), document it in the agreement and report the figures. An AI tool can show which counties use the exception and how many patients it affects. | 582 §7 second paragraph (floors may be missed if agreed in the cooperation agreement). | Treatment start |
| 3.4 | Build an early-warning dashboard on the legal clocks. Track referrals by date received and flag when the 3-week assessment or 3-month/6-month treatment clock is at risk. The clock is in law, so it is a natural alert. Use aggregate counts only; no patient-level data. | 1326/2010 §51c, §52 (clocks); 611 §7 (county must monitor and steer its own services). | Referral; diagnosis; treatment start |
| 3.5 | Use the cooperation area (five areas, each with one university hospital) as the pilot unit: one planning group, one data-sharing agreement, one dashboard. Each area has a clear hospital at the centre. | 612 §35 (cooperation areas for coordination, joint development and cooperation). | All stages |
| 3.6 | Put the cancer KPIs into the county's own financial and activity reports (toimintakertomus), which the law already requires to explain deficit-cover measures. Shows the cost of delay next to the budget. | 611 §115a (toimintakertomus must explain deficit-cover measures; [source needed]); 611 §7 (equal access). | Governance; treatment start |
| 3.7 | Use the 30-day late-payment floor (612 §61) as a shared standard for invoice ageing in cross-county cancer care. Track days-to-pay per county pair. | 612 §57, §61. | Treatment start |
| 3.8 | For a county that wants to show cancer-specific equity: use national public registers and THL indicators (aggregate only), not patient data. | Public data; no legal barrier identified in the sections read. The brief allows only aggregate public data. | All stages |

## 4. Open questions for AstraZeneca mentors

1. **Clocks.** Do counties record referral arrival dates in a form that could be checked against the 3-week and 3-month/6-month clocks in the Health Care Act (1326/2010 §51c, §52)? Or is that data missing?
2. **Cancer-specific targets.** Is there a cancer target (days from referral or diagnosis to treatment) in the national strategy or in guidelines that counties must report? Which document holds it? (UNVERIFIED that one exists in law.)
3. **Cross-county cancer care.** How often does a patient get treated out of county, and does the invoice (612 §57) cause delay in practice, or is travel the bigger barrier? Any internal data?
4. **Cooperation agreements.** Do the five cooperation areas' agreements (612 §36) already include cancer KPIs? Which area is most advanced?
5. **Volume exceptions.** Which cancer surgery volume exceptions (582 §7) have been used, and in which areas?
6. **Deficits and cancer capacity.** Which counties use the §115a extension? Are radiotherapy or imaging investments being delayed because of borrowing caps (611 §15)?
7. **FICAN.** What is its legal form and funding (UNVERIFIED), and who can commission a county pilot with it?
8. **Pilot choice.** Would AstraZeneca prefer a pilot in one cooperation area, or a national monitoring view with county drill-down?
