# 04 – Early detection and screening law (Finland)

Scope: screening law, the route to adding a new national screening, primary-care rules that affect early detection, and fee and equality rules.
Researcher: legal sub-agent, Junction 2026 AstraZeneca track. Date of research: 2026-11-13.

## Method and limits (read first)

- **Verbatim text.** Finlex pages are JavaScript-rendered. WebFetch returned only navigation for 339/2011 and 1326/2010, so I could not read the full consolidated texts. ## 2. Inefficiencies in the law that delay detection or make it unequal

Each entry gives the rule, why it hurts, the pathway stage, the evidence, and confidence.

**E1. The county's duty covers only the national programme. Other screening is optional.**
- Rule: §14 requires the national programme. Other screening is allowed ("kunta voi lisäksi"), not required.
- Why it hurts: Screening depends on each county's budget and choices. That produces the regional gaps in participation (see section 1 and Table 3 below).
- Stage: detection.
- Evidence: L1; Chancellor of Justice 2022 (L9).
- Confidence: **high** (text); **medium** (effect size).

**E2. Age bands and intervals are fixed in a government decree, not in the Act.**
- Rule: Breast 50–69 (20–26 months), cervical 30–65 (5 yrs), colorectal 56–74 (2 yrs) (L5).
- Why it hurts: Anyone outside the bands (e.g. women 40–49, men and women 75+, high-risk people under 50) gets no organised invitation. Changes need a new decree, which takes years (752/2021 and 1243/2022 are examples of amendments, each separate; text of 1243/2022 [source needed]).
- Stage: detection.
- Evidence: L5; amendment history on Finlex.
- Confidence: **high** (age bands in decree); **medium** (that no risk-based route exists is inferred from the decree text, not a stated policy).

**E3. A new national screening needs a chain of approvals with no fixed deadline.**
- Rule: Recommendation from PVN/COHERE, then STM preparation, then a government decree. The Act itself is not changed.
- Why it hurts: Lung and prostate screening have been debated for years without a national decision. Lung: a pilot that started 21.1.2026 (partly EU-funded, part of EUCanScreen, five hospitals, about 1,200 long-term smokers aged 50–74, recruited by self-referral because smoking history is not in registers; each participant takes part for two years; corrected from "2026–2028, EU-funded"). STM is still waiting for the PVN statement [source needed] (see Table 4).
- Stage: detection.
- Evidence: L8; lung pilot news items (Table 4). Prostate: an expert panel advised against a national programme, citing uncertain evidence and resource needs (statement dated 5 May 2023, STM press release 70/2023; https://valtioneuvosto.fi/-/1271139/suomalaisen-asiantuntijaryhman-kannanotto-eturauhassyovan-seulontaan-valmistunut).
- Confidence: **medium**. The process steps are from sources; the timeline and the exact PVN–decree sequence are **UNVERIFIED**.

**E4. Hoitotakuu clock for suspected cancer: no cancer-specific time limit found.**
- Rule: Under-23s: 14 days (L3). Adults: 3 months (L4). Urgent care: immediate.
- Why it hurts: An adult with a symptom that needs a scan and a specialist visit has up to 3 months under the general rule. No national clock for "suspected cancer → diagnosis" was found in statute or in STM guidance.
- Stage: primary → specialist; diagnosis.
- Evidence: L3, L4; STM 2025 guidance. The strategy (L10) mentions the aim, not a limit. The search found no cancer-specific rule (search coverage limited).
- Confidence: **medium** (absence of a rule is a search result, not proof).

**E5. The 14-day clock is met by starting the process, but STM says it must be met by delivering care.**
- Rule: STM guidance: 14 days is not met just by starting. Care measures must be carried out. Treatment should start on a working diagnosis, even before the definitive one.
- Why it hurts: Counties may count "booked" as "met". Diagnosis can stall while the clock looks fine.
- Stage: primary → specialist.
- Evidence: STM guidance 24.3.2025, p. 5 (https://stm.fi/documents/1271139/150123656/Hoitotakuuta%20koskeva%20ohje_24.3.2025.fi.pdf; checked). The 2023 document is not checked.
- Confidence: **high** (text checked in the STM 2025 guidance, p. 5).

**E6. The 14-day clock applies only to under-23s in primary care.**
- Rule: §51 a covers under-23s. Adults fall under the 3-month rule (L4).
- Why it hurts: Adult primary-care detection, where most cancer symptoms first appear, has a much looser time limit than the youth rule.
- Stage: detection (primary care).
- Evidence: L3, L4.
- Confidence: **high** (both rules as stated in sources).

**E7. Who may order lab and imaging tests in primary care: sources disagree.**
- Rule: Lab tests need a referral from a health professional. One source (Sosteri) says a nurse may refer. A public-service source (Terveyskylä) says a doctor must write it. Imaging needs a doctor's referral (HUS, Sosteri, Synlab). The statute is **not identified**.
- Why it hurts: If nurses cannot request tests, symptom-led triage stalls at the GP queue.
- Stage: detection (primary care).
- Evidence: Sosteri https://www.sosteri.fi/palvelut/terveyden-ja-sairauden-hoito/laboratoriot-rontgen-ja-kuvantaminen/ ; Terveyskylä https://www.terveyskyla.fi/tutkimukseen/tietoa-tutkimuksista/osana-hoitoa ; HUS https://diagnostiikka.hus.fi/documents/20117/87755/diagnostiikkakeskuksen_kuvantamistutkimuksiin_lahettaminen.pdf
- Confidence: **low**. The legal basis for the nurse's authority is **UNVERIFIED**.

**E8. Fee-free screening, but the fee status of follow-up is not confirmed.**
- Rule: Screening participation is free (STM; L7). Local fee guidance lists age-group screening as free (Kymenlaakso and Helsinki guides, 2024–2026; a Varha guide is cited in our notes but has no URL [source needed]).
- Why it hurts: Patients who need follow-up (a second test, a biopsy, a referral) may face fees if local rules differ. Not confirmed.
- Stage: detection → diagnosis.
- Evidence: L7; local county fee guides (e.g. https://www.hel.fi/static/sote/pysyohjeet/Terveydenhuollon%20asiakasmaksut.pdf ; https://kymenhva.fi/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Sos-ja-terv-huollon-seka-pela-asiakasmaksujen-yleiset-perusteet-2024.pdf).
- Confidence: **medium** for screening; **low / UNVERIFIED** for follow-up.

**E9. Opportunistic (non-programme) testing is locally decided and not required, so access differs by county.**
- Rule: §14 gives the county discretion. The Chancellor's 2022 decision held that prostate screening is not in the §14 duty (L9).
- Why it hurts: A man in one county may get a PSA test free through his health centre, and a man in another may not. Opportunistic tests also go outside any invitation and quality system.
- Stage: detection.
- Evidence: L1, L9.
- Confidence: **high** (legal reading); **medium** (actual county practice; not measured).

**E10. Participation gaps by education, sex and region: the law has no explicit outreach duty we could find.**
- Rule: The decree sets programmes; the Act sets the county's duty to run them. Neither, as far as we found, requires targeted outreach to low-participation groups (UNVERIFIED: whether 339/2011 sets invitation or reminder rules).
- Why it hurts: Groups with the least participation (lower education, men for colorectal, immigrants [source needed], Helsinki and some regions) are the ones most likely to be diagnosed late.
- Stage: detection.
- Evidence: Table 3.
- Confidence: **medium** (gaps are well documented in news and Syöpärekisteri summaries; the missing outreach duty is an absence-in-search finding).

## 3. Ways to work with the current law better (no law change needed)

Each item says what a county or an AI tool could do now, and the legal basis.

**W1. Run extra targeted screening in a county, under §14.**
- Action: A county runs a risk-based or outreach screen for an under-served group (e.g. men for colorectal outreach, or a lung-risk self-referral pilot like the national one).
- Basis: §14 second sentence (L1). The county may organise screening to find a specific disease or its precursor.
- Caveat: The county must still fund it; the national programme rules (intervals, ages) apply only to the national programme.
- Confidence: **high** (legal basis); **medium** (feasibility, cost not checked).

**W2. Use a county-run reminder and outreach layer on top of the national invitations.**
- Action: Track who is due (age band, last test date) from public aggregate data and send reminders or run phone outreach to the lowest-participation groups.
- Basis: operational; the decree sets who is in the programme (L5), not how the county contacts them. Whether 339/2011 sets invitation rules is UNVERIFIED.
- Confidence: **medium**.

**W3. Build an hoitotakuu watch-list for the 14-day (under-23) and 3-month (adult) clocks.**
- Action: An AI or dashboard flags patients (aggregate, not patient-level, for the demo) who are approaching day 10 of 14 (under-23) or month 2.5 of 3 (adult). It nudges the booking step and the 7-day doctor-visit rule (§51 a).
- Basis: §51 a and §50–51 (L3, L4); STM says the clock is met only when care is delivered (E5).
- Confidence: **high** (rules); **medium** (data availability at county level).

**W4. Make the nurse-to-doctor 7-day step visible.**
- Action: Track whether a referral from a non-doctor professional leads to a doctor visit within 7 days, as §51 a requires for under-23s.
- Basis: L3.
- Confidence: **high** (rule); the data feed is an operational question.

**W5. Monitor screening participation by county and education group from public statistics.**
- Action: Use Syöpärekisteri screening statistics (breast 2020–2024, cervical 2020–2024, colorectal 2022–2024 per the registry's tool [year ranges source needed]) to show gaps by region and by education. These are public, aggregate figures, so no patient-level data is needed.
- Basis: public data; the registry's own publication (https://syoparekisteri.fi/tilastot/seulontatilastot/; returns HTTP 403 in our check, so not read directly).
- Confidence: **medium** (source exists but was not read); **medium** (2024 figures from news summaries, not checked in the registry app).

**W6. Check and publish fee status of follow-up tests in each county.**
- Action: Each county's fee guide is public. Compare the guides and flag where a follow-up after a positive screen is charged.
- Basis: L7 and local fee rules (E8).
- Confidence: **medium** (guides exist; the comparison is not yet done).

**W7. Use the lung pilot as a data source, not a national programme.**
- Action: Use the pilot's public reports (when published) to model uptake and referral numbers. Do not present lung screening as a national programme; STM is still awaiting the PVN statement [source needed].
- Basis: Table 4.
- Confidence: **medium**.
