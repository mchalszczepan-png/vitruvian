# 08 – Inefficiencies: where Finnish law and rules slow or unequalise cancer care

Date: 2026-11-14. Junction 2026, AstraZeneca track. **Research only:** problems and their causes. No products, no fixes, no conclusions beyond what the evidence shows.

Built on `00-synthesis.md` and digests D01–D06, plus `07-verification.md` (which corrects several earlier clock claims), plus five new sweeps this round: client fees and travel; free choice and private care; language and migrants; county financing; cancer target days and current screening law.

## Key to tags

- **D01–D06** = `01-access-deadlines.md` … `06-workforce-strategy.md`. **07** = `07-verification.md`.
- **I-01 … I-18** = inefficiency rows in section 1.
- **Pathway steps** (deck p. 10): 1 symptoms begin · 2 deciding to seek care · 3 first contact · 4 physician assessment · 5 tests and referrals · 6 diagnosis · 7 MDT plan · 8 treatment start. Steps 1–2 are patient-side; 3–8 are system-side (my reading of the deck's bracket layout).
- **Evidence strength:** high / med / low. **Status:** VERIFIED = I read the text or page. SEARCH-SUMMARY = snippet only. UNVERIFIED = not confirmed.
- **Corrections from 07 (used below):** §50 has no day limit. §51 gives same-day contact in office hours only. §51a gives 14 days for under-23s only. The 7-day rule applies only from non-doctor to doctor. Adults get 3 months after assessment (§51a). §51c gives 3 months for specialist care, +3 if justified. §52 gives 6 months, and no "3 weeks" rule was found. §14 was amended by 375/2026 (in force 1.6.2026).

## 2. Top 5: cause chains

Ranking: strength of evidence × pathway impact × effect on equality. I-15 (medicines) ranks sixth: strong evidence, but it acts only at step 8 and is already covered in D05.

### 1. Route by payment and employment (I-12, I-09, I-10)
- **Rule:** Public primary care and occupational or private care run as separate routes. Kela private-doctor reimbursement fell to 8 € per visit from 1.1.2026 ([Kela](https://www.kela.fi/ajankohtaista/yksityislaakarikayntien-kela-korvaukset-pienenevat-1.1.2026)). Fees and travel costs apply on top.
- **Behaviour:** People with less money or no employer care start in primary care. They wait for a referral, or skip paid visits.
- **Delay or inequality:** First contact 72 vs 42 days; treatment start 207 vs 123 days (Figure 1). The paper's median test by route: 127 vs 76 days, p = 0.027 ([Kuitunen 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/cam4.71920), n = 160, Incyte-funded). Start in primary care by income: 37.5% (under 1,500 €) vs 15.4% (private/occupational).
- **Still unknown:** Whether the gap holds in a larger sample. How much is fees vs triage. How many cancer patients skip visits for money (surveys have no income split). Education was not significant in the same paper.

### 2. Clocks cover steps, not the total; nobody owns the total (I-01, I-03, I-04, I-05)
- **Rule:** Generic clocks (§51a, §51c, §52) with no cancer deadline. Monitoring is unfunded and split between FICAN and the National Cancer Centre. No public county cancer waiting data.
- **Behaviour:** Counties report each step inside its clock. No body is required to publish totals. A patient cannot see their own total.
- **Delay or inequality:** A long total wait can be fully legal. Regional gaps are invisible, so they persist.
- **Still unknown:** Total referral-to-treatment time by county and income (no series). The Ohjaus 21/2016 day values (see section 3).

### 3. Optional and uneven screening; who is missed (I-07, I-08)
- **Rule:** §14 (amended 375/2026): national programme mandatory, other screening optional. Decree 339/2011 fixes programmes and intervals. No outreach duty found.
- **Behaviour:** Counties differ in extras. Invitations reach some groups much less: Somali-born women 19% vs Finnish-born 67% (cervical, 2008–12; [Idehen 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7663516)). Somali speakers 22% vs native speakers 86% (breast imaging, 2017–18; [Fuhrmann 2024](https://medicaljournalssweden.se/actaoncologica/article/download/40830/48077/128868)).
- **Delay or inequality:** Missed screening means later diagnosis (steps 1–2 leading to 6). Stage was not measured in these studies, so the link to outcome is inferred.
- **Still unknown:** Whether the 2024 uptake figures are preliminary (the STT release does not say; D04 says they are). Whether the 2026 §14 wording changes anything. Whether a late-stage gap follows.

### 4. Fees and travel deter the first visit and repeat trips (I-09, I-10)
- **Rule:** Asiakasmaksulaki 734/1992: visit fees, day-care and inpatient-day charges. Annual ceiling about 815 € (2026, [Oikeuspalveluvirasto](https://www.oikeuspalveluvirasto.fi/en/financial-and-debt-counselling/managing-your-finances/plan-your-finances/medical-expenses/)). Waiver discretionary. Kela travel co-payments capped per year.
- **Behaviour:** Patients report avoiding care for money. 19% name finances as a barrier [source needed] (patient survey, [press release](https://www.sttinfo.fi/tiedote/70972938/kyselyselvitys-joka-kolmas-syopapotilas-kokee-diagnoosin-viivastyneen?publisherId=69820264)). 23% avoided care for possible symptoms [source needed], and 4 in 5 of them could not afford it ([Verkkouutiset](https://www.verkkouutiset.fi/a/en-halua-tietaa-moni-pelkaa-etta-oireiden-taustalla-on-syopa/), search summary).
- **Delay or inequality:** Avoidance at steps 2–3. Repeat-trip cost at step 8.
- **Still unknown:** No income-split numbers. The §6 and §6 a text was not read. No evidence on whether unpaid fees block care.

### 5. Language and residence status limit information and non-urgent work-up (I-13, I-14)
- **Rule:** Patient Act §5: information must be understood. Interpreting free only where the patient has a right to their language (Minilex). Asylum reception care under 746/2011 §26. Without residence permit, non-urgent care only if refusal is manifestly unreasonable (HE 50/2025 bill; enactment not confirmed (corrected)).
- **Behaviour:** Patients who do not understand their access date or next step miss or delay it. People without residence permit may not get non-urgent diagnostics.
- **Delay or inequality:** Delays at steps 3–5. No outcome data for these groups.
- **Still unknown:** Enacted text of HE 50/2025 and of the interpreting payer rule. How often interpreters are used in cancer pathways (not recorded).

## 3. What is still unknown (ranked)

1. **Total time from first sign to treatment start, by county and by income.** No public series. Most of the equality case depends on it.
2. **Ohjaus 21/2016 stage target days.** Every PDF copy is blocked or unreadable (07; new sweep). The 6-week figure is unresolved: a 2010 STM report or Ohjaus [source needed]. Do not put a day count on a slide.
3. **Whether the public-vs-private gap holds in a larger cohort.** Kuitunen is n = 160, 7 hospitals, one funder.
4. **Enacted text** of §14 (375/2026), §47 (913/2026, from 1.1.2027), HE 50/2025, the §6 a fee ceiling, and the Sairausvakuutuslaki travel section.
5. **Whether an outreach duty exists** for low-participation groups. None found.

## 4. Conflicts and corrections

- **[Deck](../../tracks/astrazeneca-insights-deck.md) p. 5 "public vs private":** Kuitunen p. 2 says all four primary-care providers (wellbeing regions, private, occupational, student) are publicly funded. The deck's framing should say "route and payment", not "public vs private".
- **[Deck](../../tracks/astrazeneca-insights-deck.md) p. 11:** The DLBCL values are confirmed from the figure. The colour mapping (blue private, green public) is read from the team notes, since the PDF text does not carry colours.
- **Screening "preliminary":** D04 calls the 2024 participation figures preliminary. The 2026 STT release does not say so. Treat the label as unconfirmed.
- **Residence rules:** Kela's page and HE 50/2025 describe the refusal test differently. The enacted text decides; it was not read.
- **Data availability:** [Strategy](https://stm.fi/documents/1271139/1329769/Kansallinen_syopastrategia_2026_2035_FINAL.pdf/05ad3c9d-d8ba-a629-3e90-e8d10f4f5bbc?t=1767863923329) p. 33 says monitoring lacks resources; p. 36 says reliable regional data exists. Strategy p. 35 names the National Cancer Centre as monitor; roadmap 3.1.1 names FICAN. FICAN's legal form remains unverified.
- **[Deck](../../tracks/astrazeneca-insights-deck.md) p. 11 (DLBCL):** The paper's test is by route, not by income. Education, income and occupation were not significant for delay. The deck correction also says the 7-week cut-off in Tokola et al. [source needed] is data-driven and unadjusted, and the PFS hazard ratio applies only to the high Ki-67 subgroup.
- **[Deck](../../tracks/astrazeneca-insights-deck.md) pp. 14–15 ([Zai et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjph-2025-002829)):** 27% vs 47% are crude shares of cancer deaths. The adjusted hazard ratios are 1.13 (men) and 1.14 (women), low vs high education. About 30% of cases have no stage recorded. The paper itself was not read in this round.
