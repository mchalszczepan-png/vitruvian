## 1. TL;DR

> **Update 2026-11-14:** Finlex 1326/2010 (read 2026-11-14) confirms the clocks below, including the 3-week start clock (§51c, §52). 07-verification.md says the 3-week clocks are not in law, which is wrong. 07 found **no public county-level cancer waiting-time dataset** (only a non-cancer specialist-wait proxy by wellbeing area). §14 (screening) was amended 22.5.2026/375; re-read before citing. THL Ohjaus 21/2016 day targets still unread (try section 4.3 of https://core.ac.uk/download/78470017.pdf). Concept 1 feasibility is therefore "medium-low, proxy only".
>
> **Update 2026-11-14 (data):** the gap is only cancer **waiting times**. Many other cancer indicators are public by wellbeing county (Sotkanet: 5-year survival, incidence, screening; Cancer Registry: screening by county and education, lung surgery time by cooperation area). See ../context/02-data-landscape.md.

- **The law has no cancer-specific deadline.** Cancer waits fall under the general rules (verified on Finlex, 2026-11-14, full-text search found no cancer terms in the Act: https://www.finlex.fi/fi/lainsaadanto/2010/1326): same-day contact in office hours (§51); adults 3 months after assessment, under-23 general medicine 14 days (§51a); assessment starts within 3 weeks of referral arrival (§51c, §52); specialist review or tests within 3 months (§52); specialist care 3 months, +3 if justified (§51c); treatment within 6 months of the need being established (§52). The cancer target times live in a THL guide and the strategy (https://files.hackjunction.app/resources/Kansallinen_syopastrategia_2026_2035_FINAL.pdf, p. 5), not in law. (D01, D02, D04)
- **Nobody owns the total wait.** The legal clocks are split, the start of the treatment clock is "need established" (Our reading: not a date the patient sees), and no national register requires referral or treatment-start timestamps [source needed]. A patient can be legal at every step and still wait months. (D01, D03)
- **Where you live changes what you get.** Counties choose which screenings beyond the national programme to run, which new hospital cancer drugs to adopt, and how cross-county care is agreed (renewed every council term; https://www.finlex.fi/fi/lainsaadanto/2021/612). The national strategy itself says there is no uniform procedure for new hospital medicines. (D02, D04, D05)
- **The body meant to watch equality is unfunded and has no clear legal base.** The strategy says FICAN's monitoring task has no sufficient resources, and the national quality register has no legal basis yet (strategy PDF: https://files.hackjunction.app/resources/Kansallinen_syopastrategia_2026_2035_FINAL.pdf). That data gap is the opening for an aggregate-data tool. (D06, D02)
- **A lot can be done now without changing the law.** Counties can log referral timestamps, publish cancer waits, write cancer KPIs into cooperation agreements, run targeted screening outreach under §14, and track new-drug adoption dates. A management-level, aggregate-only tool also stays clear of the medical-device and AI Act high-risk rules (reasoned, not confirmed). (D01, D02, D03, D04, D05)

## 4. Candidate product concepts (Direction A)

### Concept 1 (recommended): "Gap-to-Action Radar"

- **Pitch:** A county scorecard that shows where along the cancer pathway a county falls behind (detection, referral, diagnosis, treatment start) and hands the decision-maker three ranked actions, each with its legal basis and the source behind the number.
- **The gap it reveals:** Counties differ in screening participation and in outcomes, and the pathway timing that would explain the middle of the pathway is not published. The tool shows what is known, then shows the missing timing data as a named gap.
- **Action it recommends:** Examples: targeted colorectal outreach for men and low-education groups under §14; log and publish referral arrival timestamps (§52); write cancer KPIs into the cooperation-area agreement (612/2021 §36); offer cross-county hospital choice with the referring-doctor step built into the referral form (§47–48).
- **Data sources:** Syöpärekisteri screening statistics and regional survival and mortality (https://syoparekisteri.fi/tilastot/seulontatilastot/ ; strategy p. 35); THL/Sotkanet indicators (D06 link collection); any public hoitoonpääsy aggregates (to be confirmed); mentor input. Every figure carries a source tag and a "preliminary" flag where D04 says so.
- **Legal basis:** §14, §51c, §52, 612/2021 §36 (D01, D02, D04). Our reading: toisiolaki likely does not apply here, because only published aggregates are used (aggregate use outside the permit flow: **med**, D03 §3a; Finlex text not read, see section 6 item 8).
- **Demo feasibility:** High. The verified part (screening participation by region and group, survival and mortality) works with public data. The pathway-timing part needs a source we have not confirmed; the demo should say so.
- **Judging fit:** actionability and transparency strong; insight depends on how convincing the link between participation gaps and outcomes is (correlation, flag it).

### Concept 2: "Legal Clock Early-Warning and What-If Simulator"

- **Pitch:** Turns the legal clocks (3 weeks to assessment, §51c and §52; 3 months for specialist review, §52; 6 months to treatment, §52) and the Goal 3 target times into an early-warning view per county, with a what-if panel showing what happens to the share of cases inside target if the county logs and acts on clock risk.
- **The gap it reveals:** The law measures steps, not the total; the simulator shows the total wait a patient can legally face and how far each county is from the target.
- **Action it recommends:** Timestamp logging, clock alerts at county level, escalation to hospital choice or purchase of services elsewhere (§54 after verification), cooperation-agreement KPIs.
- **Data sources:** Statutory clock values (§51c, §52; https://www.finlex.fi/fi/lainsaadanto/2010/1326); THL Ohjaus 21/2016 targets (not read); public waiting-time aggregates (unconfirmed); explicit scenario parameters marked as assumptions.
- **Legal basis:** §51c, §52, 611/2021 §7, 612/2021 §36 (D01, D02).
- **Demo feasibility:** Medium-low. Strong visuals, but without real county timing data the simulation risks "unsupported assumptions", which the data-quality criterion (20%) penalises. Only worth it if a mentor supplies a public aggregate table.

### Concept 3: "New-Drug Adoption Lag Tracker"

- **Pitch:** For each new cancer medicine, show days from Hila decision to county adoption, by county, and which stage (Hila, Palko, county price negotiation) is the bottleneck.
- **The gap it reveals:** Equal access to new treatment fails at county adoption, not at national reimbursement (strategy p. 52: no uniform procedure).
- **Action it recommends:** Counties publish dated adoption decisions; start price negotiation earlier; use cooperation areas for shared adoption and negotiation (**UNVERIFIED** basis); document where and by whom a drug is given to avoid Kela refusals after the July 2026 change.
- **Data sources:** Hila statistics (https://www.hila.fi/toiminta-ja-organisaatio/tilastot/2024-2/), Palko recommendation list (https://palveluvalikoima.fi/valmiit-suositukset), Kela aggregate uptake, EFPIA W.A.I.T. (EU averages only).
- **Legal basis:** County adoption authority and Palko role (STM 2026 pp. 8, 14); Hila 180-day rule (D05; https://www.hila.fi/toiminta-ja-organisaatio/usein-kysytyt-kysymykset/).
- **Demo feasibility:** Low-medium. County adoption dates are not confirmed public. Also a neutrality risk: a medicines-access tool fits the sponsor's commercial interest, so every claim must be shown with its source and limits. Better as a module inside Concept 1 than a stand-alone product.
