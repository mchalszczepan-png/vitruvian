## 2. Inefficiencies in the law that hurt cancer patients

Each row: rule → why it hurts cancer care → pathway stage → evidence → confidence.

**2.1 No cancer-specific deadline; generic outer limits only**
- **Rule:** Specialised care must start "kohtuullisessa ajassa", within 6 months of the need being established (§52). Assessment starts within 3 weeks (§52, §51c), and specialist review or imaging/lab tests must be done within 3 months (§52).
- **Why it hurts:** A suspected or confirmed cancer can wait up to 6 months under the statute before treatment is legally late. The strategy's target times are not in the Act (no cancer term in the full text).
- **Stage:** Diagnosis → treatment start.
- **Evidence:** §52 text and the full-text check (https://www.finlex.fi/fi/lainsaadanto/2010/1326, 2026-11-14). Strategy p. 5 indicator, which names THL Ohjaus 21/2016 as the measure; the target values are **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Confidence:** High (text). Medium (that no cancer deadline exists in decrees or guidance; not searched).

**2.2 The clocks are split and the total wait is not set in law**
- **Rule:** Three separate clocks: (a) referral arrival → assessment start, 3 weeks (§52, §51c); (b) assessment → treatment, 6 months from the need being established (§52), or 3 months from the assessment for primary-care-linked specialist care, +3 months if justified (§51c); (c) specialist review or imaging/lab tests done within 3 months of referral arrival (§52). The start of clock (b) is "todettu" (established). §55 publishes compliance per time limit, not a total.
- **Why it hurts:** No statute sets a total referral-to-treatment limit (text checked 2026-11-14). So a patient can be "within the law" at every step and still wait many months. Specialist review or imaging extends the first clock to 3 months (§52). Whether that is the typical cancer work-up is **[source needed]**.
- **Stage:** Referral → diagnosis → treatment start.
- **Evidence:** §51c, §52, §55 (Finlex, above). §4 potilaslaki requires the treatment access date to be given, but sets no maximum (Finlex 785/1992).
- **Confidence:** High (text). Medium (practical effect on cancer; this is our inference).

**2.3 "Kiireellinen" is defined around acute deterioration, so a suspected but non-acute cancer has no urgent lane**
- **Rule:** §50 defines urgent care as the immediate assessment and treatment of acute illness, injury, worsening of a chronic condition, or loss of functional capacity, which cannot be delayed without worsening.
- **Why it hurts:** Our reading: a cancer referral for suspicion without acute symptoms is not urgent by that definition. It goes to the ordinary queue (§51a, §52) with the 3-week and 3-month/6-month clocks. The brief's "urgent" category is therefore not a cancer category in law.
- **Stage:** Detection → referral.
- **Evidence:** §50 text (https://www.finlex.fi/fi/lainsaadanto/2010/1326).
- **Confidence:** High (definition). Medium (that no separate urgent cancer referral category exists in practice; **UNVERIFIED** against Käypä hoito or county practice).

**2.4 Same-day contact is only during office hours; adult primary-care first treatment has a 3-month outer limit**
- **Rule:** Same-day contact with a unit and an individual assessment is guaranteed only during weekday office hours (§51). For adults with a non-urgent primary-care need, the first treatment event must be within 3 months of assessment (§51a). The 14-day window applies only to under-23s with general-medicine needs. The 7-day window applies only to a referral from a non-doctor professional to a doctor (§51a).
- **Why it hurts:** Our reading: symptoms that prompt a cancer check will often reach adults over 23 through the ordinary queue, with a 3-month outer limit **[source needed: share of cancer referrals that take this route]**. §51 does not guarantee same-day contact outside office hours; urgent out-of-hours care is under §50. The 7-day rule covers only one handoff.
- **Stage:** Detection (symptom → primary care).
- **Evidence:** §50, §51, §51a (Finlex, above).
- **Confidence:** High (text).

**2.5 The 2023 "7-day" rule and its later loosening: only partly verified**
- **Rule:** In the current §51a text (checked 2026-11-14), the 7-day window applies only to a referral from a non-doctor professional to a doctor. The 14-day window applies to under-23s with a general-medicine need.
- **Why it hurts:** The current text does not give adults with cancer suspicion a 7-day doctor contact. This is our reading.
- **The 2023 plan:** A government release headline says the primary-care guarantee would tighten to two weeks from September 2023 ([headline, not opened](https://valtioneuvosto.fi/-/1271139/perusterveydenhuollon-hoitotakuu-tiukkenee-kahteen-viikkoon-syyskuussa-2023-)). An Edilex summary ([Edilex 20240134](https://www.edilex.fi/he/20240134), read with WebFetch on 2026-11-14 per 07-verification.md) reports that the change was not put into force. The exact content of the 2023 plan is **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Stage:** Detection → primary care.
- **Confidence:** Medium (current text verified; history **UNVERIFIED**).

**2.6 Choice of hospital requires the referring doctor's agreement; patients lack information to choose**
- **Rule:** Patients can choose a specialised unit across county lines, but only in agreement with the referring doctor (§47, §48). Choice of health centre has a 1-year re-choice limit until 1.1.2027, then 3 months (§47, amended 913/2026).
- **Why it hurts:** The right to choose is real, but it depends on a doctor's agreement. §55 publishes time-limit compliance per unit, not cancer waits, so the patient has no cancer-specific figure to choose by. Our reading: patients may lack the waiting-time information they would need.
- **Stage:** Referral.
- **Evidence:** §47–48 and §55 (Finlex, above).
- **Confidence:** High (text). Low (on the practical information gap).

**2.7 No statutory remedy tied to a missed date; complaints route is generic**
- **Rule:** §4 potilaslaki says the date must be given and changes explained. It does not say what follows if the date is missed. Complaint routes: §10 muistutus (written answer from the unit within a reasonable time) and §10a kantelu (to the supervisory authority, under the administrative complaint rules). §15 (appeal) was not read.
- **Why it hurts:** Our reading: a patient who is told a date and then waits longer has no clear, fast remedy in the text. Cancer patients often cannot wait for a complaint process **[source needed]**.
- **Stage:** Treatment start.
- **Evidence:** §4, §10, §10a (Finlex 785/1992, checked 2026-11-14).
- **Confidence:** High (§4, §10, §10a text). Low (on how fast any remedy works; §15 not read).

**2.8 Strategy target times exist as an indicator but not as values**
- **Rule:** Strategy Goal 3 indicator: "Potilaat pääsevät hoitoon tavoiteaikojen puitteissa" (strategy p. 5). The footnote refers to THL Ohjaus 21/2016, which defines the measurement points: referral arrival at specialised care → first treatment procedure, grouped by cancer type (search summary; document not opened).
- **Why it hurts:** The target is not measurable without the Ohjaus 21/2016 values. Those values were not found.
- **Stage:** Referral → treatment start.
- **Evidence:** strategy p. 5; WebSearch summary of THL Ohjaus 21/2016 (possible copy at https://core.ac.uk/download/78470017.pdf, search summary only; PDF not opened).
- **Confidence:** Medium (indicator text verified; the document's content not verified).

## 3. Ways to work with the current law better

Each item needs no law change. Legal basis is given for each.

| # | Action (who could do it now) | Legal basis | Pathway stage |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | **Log the referral arrival timestamp and assessment date for every cancer-suspicion referral.** The §52 and §51c clocks start at arrival. Without the timestamp, compliance cannot be shown. (County IT, referral system.) | §52, §51c | Referral |
| 3.2 | **Run a clock dashboard on the three legal clocks per county and per cancer type**: 3 weeks to assessment, 3 months for work-up, 6 months to treatment. Aggregate only, no patient-level data. The §55 per-unit figures are the likely input; whether they are published, and where, is not checked. Adding cancer type closes gap 2.2 without changing the law. (County, THL or an AI analyst on public aggregates.) | §52, §51c, §55; strategy p. 5 indicator | Referral → treatment |
| 3.3 | **Tell each patient their treatment date, and tell them when it changes and why.** §4 already requires this. A checklist in the referral letter with the date and reason covers it. (County clinics.) | §4 potilaslaki | Treatment start |
| 3.4 | **Give patients a plain-language summary of options and what each means for timing**, including the choice of hospital across county lines. (County patient info, AI-drafted and clinician-checked.) | §5 potilaslaki (highlights); §47–48 | Referral |
| 3.5 | **Offer the cross-county hospital choice explicitly, with the referring doctor's agreement, when the home county's wait is long.** The agreement step can be built into the referral form. (GP and referral team.) | §47 para 2, §48 | Referral |
| 3.6 | **Use the 14-day and 7-day short windows where they apply, and flag where they do not.** A triage tool can identify cases that fall under §51a 7-day (non-doctor → doctor) and 14-day (under-23) and route them. For adults with suspicion, flag for clinical review of §50 urgency, since §50 covers acute deterioration only. (Primary care triage.) | §50, §51, §51a | Detection → referral |
| 3.7 | **Make out-of-hours contact a service question.** §51 guarantees same-day contact only in office hours, so a county can offer out-of-hours symptom contact as a service to cover that gap. (County primary care.) | §51 | Detection |
| 3.8 | **Ask counties to split the §55 publication by cancer type.** §55 already requires monthly per-unit publication of time-limit compliance, but it is not cancer-specific. (County, THL.) | §55 (text checked 2026-11-14) | All stages |
| 3.9 | **Use §54 as the escalation step when a clock is breached.** If the county cannot meet the limits itself, §54 requires it to arrange care jointly with another county or buy it from other providers. (County management.) | §54 (text checked 2026-11-14) | Treatment start |
