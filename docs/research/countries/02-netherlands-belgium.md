# Netherlands and Belgium: cancer registry, waits, fast lanes, equity

Scope: Netherlands (NL) and Belgium (BE). Research only, no code.
Date of research: 2026-11-14.

## How to read the tags

- **READ**: I read the page or abstract (or the tool's full highlight text).
- **SNIPPET**: I only saw a search summary. Treat as a lead, not proof.
- **UNVERIFIED**: from memory or a secondary summary. Do not quote without checking.
- **[source needed]**: a claim I could not link to a source after two searches. Kept, not deleted.
- **Our reading**: our judgement, not a sourced claim. The "Confidence" column is also our judgement.

## Acronyms (explained once)

- **NKR**: Netherlands Cancer Registry. **NCR** is the same thing in English sources.
- **IKNL**: Netherlands Comprehensive Cancer Organisation. It runs the NKR.
- **PALGA**: Dutch national pathology archive. Labs send every pathology report to it.
- **BCR**: Belgian Cancer Registry.
- **MOC / MOC-COM**: Belgian multidisciplinary oncology consultation (tumour board).
- **Kankerregister**: Dutch-language name of the Belgian registry (our reading: same body as the BCR).
- **DICA**: Dutch Institute for Clinical Auditing. **DSCA** is its colorectal audit.
- **NZa**: Dutch healthcare authority. It publishes hospital wait-time data.
- **Treeknormen**: agreed Dutch maximum waits (not law).
- **TNM / pTNM / cTNM**: tumour size, nodes, spread staging. "p" is from pathology, "c" is clinical.
- **ECIS**: European Cancer Information System. Its stage-availability study is used below.
- **SES**: socio-economic status.
- **EHR**: electronic health record.

## Initiative details

### 1. Netherlands Cancer Registry (NKR, IKNL)

- **What it is**: nationwide registry since 1989. Covers all cancer patients. SNIPPET ([PMC4595560](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4595560/)).
- **How cases are found**: PALGA pathology reports, plus a hospital database for cases without pathology. SNIPPET ([Tijdschrift voor Urologie](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13629-021-00318-x)).
- **How stage is got**: trained, independent data managers read the electronic patient file with a strict coding manual. SNIPPET (same source). Stage codes: "unknown" when TNM cannot be worked out (e.g. TX/NX/M0); "missing" when no TNM was recorded at all (this can mean EoD staging was used instead). SNIPPET ([IKNL, NCR](https://iknl.nl/en/ncr)).
- **Completeness**: complete TNM over 90% for five cancers (breast, colorectal, lung, prostate, cervix); over 80% for gastric (2017-19, ECIS). READ ([PMC13595485](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13595485/)). (Corrected: the earlier text said "six cancers over 90%". Gastric is 80%.) ECIS counts a case "complete" when M1 is known, even if T and N are missing. Breakdown of unknown and missing is in supplementary tables I could not read.
- **Timeliness**: data are not real time. Vital status comes from the municipal personal records database. IKNL's [NCR data page](https://iknl.nl/en/ncr-data) (SNIPPET) says the last vital-status update used data to 31 January 2024 and the next was planned for summer 2025. The yearly refresh claim is not confirmed: [source needed]. The Datagateway project aims to move closer to real time (READ, [PMC12303076](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12303076/)).
- **Legal and funding basis**: not verified. Hospitals have contracts with NKR (SNIPPET, [palga.nl](https://palga.nl/en_GB/nkr-reporting)). I did not find the statute or the funding split. UNVERIFIED.

### 2. PALGA automated feed

- **Did it**: diagnoses flow in electronically from PALGA (SNIPPET, [palga.nl](https://palga.nl/en_GB/nkr-reporting)). PALGA runs an alert system that flags odd or missing data: [source needed] (SNIPPET only, no link confirmed).
- **Automation level**: skin squamous cell cancer is imported without manual entry since mid-2016. SNIPPET ([PMC13332985](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13332985/)).
- **Datagateway pilot** (READ, [PMC12303076](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12303076/)): reads structured EHR data across hospitals.
  - 100% match to NKR diagnoses for 4 cancers (AML, myeloma, lung, breast).
  - 95% accuracy on new diagnoses against NKR inclusion rules.
  - Treatment identified correctly; 3% of combination therapies misclassified.
  - Toxicity indicators 0% to 100% accuracy (weak for some).
  - Stage not reported in the highlights I read.
- **Lesson** (our reading): automatic pathology feed plus structured EHR data is feasible. It is a pilot on 4 cancers, not proof for stage.

### 3. Dutch waiting-time norms (Treeknormen)

- **Norms**: 4 weeks for first clinic visit or diagnostics; 7 weeks for treatment. Agreed by providers and insurers, not law. SNIPPET ([avl.nl](https://avl.nl/en/preparing-for-your-appointment/wait-times)).
- **GP norm**: appointment within 2 days in 80% of cases: [source needed] (the earlier summary did not give a link).
- **Public data**: NZa publishes hospital wait data (NZa page itself: [source needed]). A [Kassa article, 30 Dec 2024](https://www.bnnvara.nl/kassa/artikelen/doorverwezen-naar-een-medisch-specialist-reken-op-enorme-wachttijden) (READ) reports that 86.4% of maag-, darm- en leverziekten clinics exceed the 4-week norm (average overrun 13.2 weeks; average total wait 17.2 weeks). The Independer analysis is cited there, not reproduced.
- **Gap** (our reading): norms are per step. They do not measure the total wait from GP referral to treatment, which is problem 1 for Finland.
- **Time to diagnosis study**: Helsper et al. 2017, European Journal of Cancer vol. 87, pp. 113-121 ([Utrecht repository](https://dspace.library.uu.nl/handle/1874/357969), SNIPPET; full PDF [here](https://dspace.library.uu.nl/bitstream/1874/357969/1/1_s2.0_S0959804917313436_main.pdf)). (Corrected: the DOI in an earlier draft, S0959-8049(17)31343-6, returns 404 at doi.org. Not used.) Design (SNIPPET): retrospective cohort of GP records linked to the NKR; breast, colorectal, lung, prostate and melanoma. Medians from the abstract fragments (SNIPPET, check the PDF before quoting): breast 7 days, melanoma 21, lung 49, colorectal 54, prostate 137. About 10-25% of patients had a substantially prolonged interval. For colorectal cancer, the GP share of the delay grew as the total interval lengthened.
- **Survival vs delay**: mixed. Elit et al. 2014 is a uterine cancer study (J Clin Oncol 2014;32:27-33, "Impact of wait times on survival for women with uterine cancer"), not cervix (corrected). Simunovic et al. 2009 is a colon cancer study: disease-specific survival was not affected by the consult-to-admission wait (HR 1.0, 95% CI 0.9-1.2) (SNIPPET). Neither paper link: [source needed]. The earlier "cervix, waits over 12 weeks" description was wrong.

### 4. DICA / DSCA audits

- **Did it**: yes. The colorectal audit (DSCA) started in 2009 per [PubMed 23871573](https://www.pubmed.gov/23871573/) (SNIPPET). Other sources give 2010 or 2011: [source needed]. Full participation. Weekly MyDICA feedback. Risk-adjusted hospital benchmarks ([DICA PDF](https://connect.ichom.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/2.-Building-National-Outcomes-Registries-in-the-Netherlands-The-Dutch-Institute-for-Clinical-Auditing-DICA-1.pdf), SNIPPET).
- **Outcomes claimed**: lower postoperative death and illness; 30-day death lower after laparoscopic colorectal surgery; lower margin involvement in rectal surgery. SNIPPET (Leiden dissertations: [source needed]).
- **Policy effect**: audit found overtreatment of early rectal cancer; guideline revised. SNIPPET: [source needed] for link.
- **Evidence strength** (our reading, based on search summaries): most claims come from DICA-linked authors using audit data. Search results on audit and feedback in surgery (no full text read; links [source needed]): a review of 5 studies comparing audit plus quality improvement with audit alone (4 showed an added effect); a review of 17 surgeon-feedback studies (16 non-randomised, 1 randomised), where the non-randomised studies support improvement and the one randomised study suggests no effect. Evidence is mostly before-and-after studies.
- **Relevance to stage**: DICA audits use NKR checks for concordance (SNIPPET: [source needed] for link). Not a stage intervention.

### 5. Dutch GP gatekeeping and fast diagnostics

- **Gatekeeping**: GP referral needed for specialist care, except emergencies. SNIPPET: [source needed] for link.
- **Direct access (UK evidence, not Dutch)**: Smith et al. 2018, British Journal of General Practice 68(674): e594-e603 ([BJGP](https://bjgp.org/content/68/674/e594), SNIPPET). 60 papers; most UK-based and judged poor quality. Cancer detection from GP direct-access tests did not differ from specialist-triaged testing, except for gastroscopy. Referral-to-test was faster (mean 31.9 days vs 76.6 days, Pulse summary, SNIPPET). Time to diagnosis did not fall. (Corrected: an earlier draft called this a Dutch review.)
- **NICE standard** (England, for comparison): QS124 on direct access ([nice.org.uk](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/qs124), SNIPPET).
- **Sneldiagnostiek**: no evaluation found. Only a student thesis (57 patients returned, 27 complete) ([UTwente](https://essay.utwente.nl/fileshare/file/70845/Sturtz%20en%20Geuke_BA_TNW.pdf), SNIPPET). Did not check the NHG (Dutch GP guideline) text, which I could not reach.
- **Gut feeling**: Donker et al. (HW 2017, [PDF](https://www.henw.org/system/files/download/HW60-620.pdf), READ). GP gut feeling is a better predictor with experience. Companion paper "Bijblijven 2018": [source needed].

### 6. Belgian Cancer Registry (BCR)

- **Mandate**: notification by oncology care programmes and pathology labs. A 2006 law made registration compulsory ([EMA catalogue](https://catalogues.ema.europa.eu/node/4943/data-flows-and-management), SNIPPET). Compulsory insurance covers all residents, so selection bias is low (SNIPPET, [PMC8411068](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8411068/)). The 2006 law text itself is UNVERIFIED.
- **Refresh and scope**: one EMA catalogue entry says the data are refreshed yearly and can be checked against medical charts (SNIPPET). Its OMOP mapping covers only breast and lung cancer, incidence years 2014-2023 (SNIPPET).
- **Completeness**: about 95% from 2004 (SNIPPET, [PMC8411068](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8411068/)). The ECIS figure of over 90% complete TNM for 2017-19 is read for NL ([PMC13595485](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13595485/)). The Belgian value: [source needed].
- **Timing**: clinical data from oncology care programmes arrive about 6 months after the year of diagnosis. Stage cannot be assessed without it (SNIPPET, PMC8411068; our reading on the consequence).
- **Stage source**: the MOC / oncology programme form (MOC-COM). The INAMI page links the MOC fee to the registration form ([INAMI](https://www.inami.fgov.be/nl/professionals/individuele-zorgverleners/artsen/zorg-door-artsen/multidisciplinair-oncologisch-consult-moc), SNIPPET). Hospital data managers extract data from medical files (SNIPPET, PMC12875157 snippet). Hospital data managers paid by hospitals: [source needed].
- **Compare with NL** (our reading): both countries have strong completeness. NL uses one central registrar team. BE uses hospital-paid data managers plus lab feeds, and clinical data arrive later.

### 7. Belgian MOC (tumour boards)

- **Coverage**: 90.4% of new cancer patients discussed at MDT (page read in an earlier pass; not reconfirmed in the second pass; 2021 year: [source needed]). Target 90% (KCE excerpt, SNIPPET).
- **Trend** (KCE report, 2019 performance report, SNIPPET; [source needed] for link): 50.8% in 2004 (the first full year after financing started), 83.6% in 2012. Breast cancer reached 95.7% in the last column of the KCE table; digestive and respiratory sites were below 90% (SNIPPET). (Corrected: the earlier 52.5% (2004) and 78.9% (2010) figures are not in the excerpt.)
- **Financing**: started around 2003 (KCE excerpt, SNIPPET: [source needed] for link).
- **Quality indicator QC-6**: the KCE definition counts patients with an invasive cancer diagnosed in a year, first tumour only, discussed at a multidisciplinary meeting in hospital care (SNIPPET; indicator sheet [qc6.pdf](https://www.healthybelgium.be/metadata/hspa/qc6.pdf), PDF not readable in our pass). The data source named as the BCR in an earlier search summary: [source needed].
- **Link to stage**: MOC is a route for clinical data into the BCR (INAMI, SNIPPET). No study I found measures whether MOC raised stage completeness or lowered stage at diagnosis.

### 8. Belgian equity by SES

- **Survival, colon and lung** (Rosskamp et al., [ENCR 2018 slides](https://www.encr.eu/sites/default/files/2018-ENCR-Conference/Michael%20Rosskamp%20-%20ENCR2018%20-%20impact%20of%20SE%20factors%20on%20survival%20in%20Belgium.pdf), SNIPPET): BCR incidence data 2006-2013. Survival differs by income, household and marital status (corrected: the earlier text left out marital status). No urban-rural gap. The slide charts are images; effect sizes not read.
- **Income and survival, eight cancers** (SNIPPET, [search record](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/4MzoMwxl), title "Social Inequalities in Cancer Survival in Belgium: A Population-Based Cohort Study"): 109,591 patients diagnosed 2006-2013 with one of eight common cancers. Lower income, unemployment and living alone were linked to worse survival (corrected: the earlier "low and middle income" wording was not from the summary). Stage adjustment result not checked.
- **Stage gap** (Rosskamp et al., Cancers 2021, [DOI 10.3390/cancers13050933](https://doi.org/10.3390/cancers13050933), SNIPPET; [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33668089/)): the 2001 census (adults 25 and over, about 6.96 million) was linked to BCR diagnoses 2004-2013 for six cancers (lung, colon, rectal, head and neck, female breast, melanoma). Lower SES was linked to lower odds of a known or early stage. The strongest disparities were male lung cancer and female breast cancer.
- **Breast, education** (Gotink et al., [BMC Cancer, Nov 2024](https://doaj.org/article/57989c1e265d46558d4afb781825fb99), SNIPPET, not read in full): the education gap stays after stage adjustment. Stage explains part of the gap.
- **Caveat** (our reading): measures differ (income, education, household). Do not pool numbers.

## Transferable to Finland (our reading, not sourced claims)

- **Use a registry-run stage team, or a pathology-plus-clinical feed.** NL gets over 90% complete stage for five cancers, and over 80% for gastric (ECIS, READ), using trained abstractors plus automatic pathology feeds. Finland could start with pathology (PALGA-like feed) and add a clinical notification with a short stage form, so stage does not depend only on the clinical notification. Finland's notification share is about 40-50% of cases, not of hospitals: "vain noin 40 %" in the 2023 registry report ([archived copy](https://web.archive.org/web/2025/https://syoparekisteri.fi/assets/files/2025/06/Syopa_2023_fi.pdf)) and "under half for almost ten years" in the [2025 registry statement](https://syoparekisteri.fi/tietoa-syoparekisterista/tietotilinpaatos-2025/). Detail: [07-stage-data-practice.md](../context/07-stage-data-practice.md).
- **Make the clinical notification part of a meeting that already happens.** Belgium's MOC form is the clearest example (INAMI, SNIPPET). The Finnish equivalent could be the tumour-board or MDT record, if it is coded.
- **Measure time in steps, not one number.** NL norms (4 weeks for diagnosis, 7 weeks for treatment) are per step (avl.nl, SNIPPET). Problem 1 needs a start-to-treatment clock. NL does not have that across the whole route either.
- **Audit with feedback, and check it against the registry.** DSCA plus NKR concordance (SNIPPET) shows a way to build trust in the numbers. Keep the claim modest: audit evidence is not independent.
- **Watch the lag.** Belgium's clinical data arrive about 6 months late (SNIPPET, PMC8411068). Any Finnish fast-lane data must be near real time to be useful.
- **Track two-speed entry from the start.** Belgium has SES gaps in stage and survival (SNIPPET). Add income and education fields to the registry linkage so the gap is measured, not guessed.
