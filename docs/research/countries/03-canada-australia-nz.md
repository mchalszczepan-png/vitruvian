# Canada (Ontario), Australia, New Zealand: cancer waits, fast lanes, stage capture

Date of research: 2026-11-14. Scope: Ontario, Australia, New Zealand.

**Problems (from the brief):** P1 = nobody measures the total cancer wait (referral to treatment). P3 = no fast lane for suspected cancer from public GP. P4 = stage at diagnosis missing for ~58% of Finnish registry cases. Lens = two-speed entry (lower-income people start in the slow public route).

**Tags:** READ = I read the page or abstract text. SNIPPET = only a search summary. UNVERIFIED = memory or secondary, not checked. [source needed] = claim kept, no URL found after two searches.

**Acronyms:** DAP = Diagnostic Assessment Program (Ontario one-stop lung/breast/colorectal clinic). CSQI = Cancer System Quality Index (Ontario). OCP = Optimal Care Pathway (Australia). FCT = Faster Cancer Treatment (NZ). RD-Stage = registry-derived stage. MDM = multidisciplinary meeting. CCO = Cancer Care Ontario (now part of Ontario Health). NSCLC = non-small cell lung cancer.

**Important correction to the brief:** NZ's headline measure is now the 31-day target (decision to treat to first treatment, 90% goal). The brief says the 62-day benchmark was replaced by the 31-day indicator in 2012. That date is not confirmed (corrected). The 62-day indicator was still reported for 2020-21 ([HDC decision 2023](https://hdc.org.nz/decisions/search-decisions/2023/22hdc01310), SNIPPET), and the 2024 health targets list only the 31-day measure ([health targets](https://www.health.govt.nz/monitoring-statistics/system-monitoring/health-targets), SNIPPET). Use 31-day as the model.

## 1. Ontario, Canada

### 1a. Lung Diagnostic Assessment Program (DAP): P3 fast lane

- **Did it:** Yes. Each Ontario regional cancer programme offers a lung DAP. About 45% of lung patients went through one in 2020 (SNIPPET, [Healthcare Quarterly, January 2020](https://www.longwoods.com/content/26082)). No current provincial figure found, so treat 45% as a dated estimate (corrected: the brief implies current data). Provincial target: 65% of patients from referral to tissue diagnosis within 28 days (SNIPPET, same source). A Southeastern Ontario study reports the target was met for fewer than 40% of lung DAP patients in 2018 (SNIPPET, [source needed]).
- **Key study:** Habbous et al., "The effect of diagnostic assessment programs on the diagnosis and treatment of patients with lung cancer in Ontario, Canada." *Annals of Thoracic Medicine*, 2021. [DOI 10.4103/atm.atm_283_20](https://doi.org/10.4103/atm.atm_283_20). DOI verified (verify_reference: verified). Abstract text read at https://www.ovid.com/jnls/aotm/fulltext/10.4103/atm.atm_283_20~the-effect-of-diagnostic-assessment-programs-on-the (READ, via highlights).
  - Early-stage disease: 36% of DAP patients vs 25% of non-DAP (READ, [Habbous 2021](https://doi.org/10.4103/atm.atm_283_20)).
  - Time from first health-care contact to diagnosis: median 64 days DAP vs 61 days non-DAP. After adjustment, no difference (beta -0.8 days) (READ, same).
  - Time to treatment: 8.5 days shorter for DAP patients (READ, same).
  - Overall survival: adjusted HR 0.79 for DAP. The gain disappeared after adjusting for emergency presentation (HR 0.96) (READ, same).
  - Guideline benchmarks cited: suspicion to diagnosis 28 days; diagnosis to treatment 4 to 6 weeks (READ, same).
- **Reading:** Our reading: The DAP speeds up work-up after diagnosis but did not shorten the wait to diagnosis. The survival gain looks like a case-mix effect. This is an observational comparison, not a randomised trial.
- **Lens check:** Income or two-speed entry results were not found in the sources read. Gap.

### 1b. Ontario wait-time and performance reporting: P1

- **Did it:** Partly. Regional Cancer Program (RCP) scorecard for 14 regional programmes, reviewed quarterly by CCO (SNIPPET; [RCP scorecard](https://hsihelp.ontariohealth.ca/hsi/RCP_Scorecard/RCP_Scorecard.htm) and [2025/26 methodology](https://hsihelp.ontariohealth.ca/hsi/assets/misc_doc/2025-26_Indicator_Methodology_March%202025.pdf), SNIPPET). Older descriptions (2013, 2015, 2019) also exist (SNIPPET, [source needed]).
- Surgery wait indicator "percentage of cancer surgeries completed within target" exists in the Ontario Health indicator library, reported by hospital (SNIPPET, [indicator library](https://indicatorlibrary.hqontario.ca/Indicator/Summary/Percentage-cancer-surgeries-completed-within-target/EN)). The indicator library was not reachable when first read (DNS error).
- Funding link is disputed. A 2015 poster says no funding tied to scorecard; a 2004-era deck lists quality-linked funding (SNIPPET, [source needed]). A later search summary says funding is not directly tied, though one region's scorecard rank was linked to executive compensation in 2015 (SNIPPET, [source needed]). Treat as unresolved.
- Cancer Quality Council of Ontario publicly reports on system performance (SNIPPET, [source needed]).
- **Outcome evidence:** None found that the scorecard reduced waits.
- Sudbury Today reported (SNIPPET, [source needed]) that Ontario Health had not published the latest 2023 surgery wait statistics at the time of the article. Date of article not checked.

### 1c. Stage capture for the Ontario Cancer Registry: P4

- **Did it:** Yes. CCO collects stage elements from patient records via remote access to regional centres and community hospitals, using Collaborative Staging (SNIPPET, [source needed]; CCO staging page was blocked, WEBSITE_BLOCKED).
- In 2009 CCO ran a Stage Capture and Pathology Reporting Project to standardise pathology e-reports to the registry. Vendor certification was part of this (SNIPPET, [overview PDF](https://ktecop.ca/wp-content/uploads/cco-stage-path-project-overview-jun-2009.pdf)).
- **Completeness:**
  - NSCLC cohort, 51,152 patients: 11.2% (5,707) unstaged. Unstaged patients were older, lower income, more comorbid, and 89.4% died within 4 years (READ, [Frontiers 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/oncology/articles/10.3389/fonc.2023.1146053/full), published 2023-04-04). Authors conclude stage data is not missing completely at random.
  - Synoptic pathology completeness 87% in 2013 data (SNIPPET, CCO technical appendix, [source needed]). Date is old.
- **Reading:** Our reading: Ontario is not at full completeness even with 20 years of work. The missing group is also the sickest and poorest. That is a useful finding for P4 and the two-speed lens.

## 2. Australia

### 2a. Optimal Care Pathways (OCPs) with time frames: P1

- **Did it:** Yes. Cancer Council Australia publishes tumour-specific OCPs with time targets (SNIPPET; https://www.cancer.org.au/health-professionals/optimal-cancer-care-pathways.html not read).
- Lung OCP (2014) target: first specialist appointment within 14 days of referral; first treatment within 42 days of referral (SNIPPET, search summary of Cancer Council Victoria 2014 pathway, [source needed]).
- **Evidence (SNIPPET only, Victorian and other studies, different periods and cancers):**
  - Corrected: the 93% seen by respiratory service within 14 days of GP referral and 71% diagnosed within 28 days come from a Tasmanian study with results pooled across years, not a 2019 to 2022 Victorian audit (SNIPPET, [source needed]). Both attributions were wrong in the earlier draft.
  - 2018 Victorian audit, oesophagogastric cancer: 40% started treatment within 28 days of diagnosis; 26% had MDM to treatment within 14 days (SNIPPET, [source needed]).
  - Pancreatic cancer (Peter Mac, 1,878 patients): median 20 days diagnosis to surgery (benchmark 28 days), 60% on time (SNIPPET, [source needed]).
  - A Victorian Integrated Cancer Service conference abstract (2015, SNIPPET) reports about 70% of lung diagnoses confirmed within 28 days of referral and 50% of patients from diagnosis to first treatment within 14 days, [source](https://vics-2015.p.asnevents.com.au/days/2015-05-12/abstract/23443).
- **Reading:** Our reading: Diagnosis-to-treatment and MDM-to-treatment are the most-missed steps. Early specialist access is generally met.

### 2b. Nurse navigator intervention (Monash Health, 2025): P1 fix

- SNIPPET only. In a 2025 COSA/IPOS conference abstract (Monash Health), clinical nurse navigators were added to lung and gastrointestinal cancer clinics. The mean wait from "ready for care" to chemotherapy fell from 45 to 23 days (corrected: the abstract text read gives this as one combined result, not two cohorts; the second figure, 47 to 32 days, was not found and needs [source needed]). Source: [COSA/IPOS 2025 abstract](https://cosa-ipos-2025.p.asnevents.com.au/days/2025-11-14/abstract/126636). Single site; retrospective audit, no control group.

### 2c. National stage capture (RD-Stage, Cancer Australia STaR project): P4

- **Did it:** Yes. All state and territory population-based registries produce RD-Stage using endorsed national business rules (SNIPPET, [source needed]). Simplified AJCC TNM rules were developed by the Victorian Cancer Registry (SNIPPET, [source needed]).
- **Completeness, 2011 diagnoses:** melanoma 97%, prostate 97%, female breast 94%, colorectal 88%, lung 72% (SNIPPET, STaR project paper, exact URL [source needed]; the Cancer Australia table itself was not read).
- Other cancers: endometrial completeness ranged 0% to 89% across jurisdictions; pancreatic 9% to 76% (SNIPPET, [source needed]).
- **Reading:** Our reading: Registrar-coded stage works best for cancers where stage rules are simple. Lung is hardest.
- Victoria or NSW "STaging Project": not found under that name.

### 2d. OCP for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people: equity

- **Did it:** Yes. First population-specific OCP for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people with cancer, endorsed nationally, built on the National Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Cancer Framework ([Chynoweth et al., JCO Global Oncology 2020](https://doaj.org/article/8360b42eff2e443fbf975f9bc94f85e1), SNIPPET). Sources differ on the exact endorsing bodies (SNIPPET). Indigenous Australians face higher cancer mortality (SNIPPET, same source: about 40% more likely to die).
- **Evidence:** No outcome data found. Tool is for gap analysis and quality improvement (SNIPPET, same source).

## 3. New Zealand

### 3a. Faster Cancer Treatment (FCT) 31-day target: P1

- **Did it:** Yes. Measure: share of eligible patients who get first treatment within 31 days of a health professional's decision to treat (SNIPPET, [Health NZ factsheet](https://www.tewhatuora.govt.nz/assets/Corporate-information/Planning-and-performance/Health-targets/Quarter-4-2024-25/Health-targets-Factsheets.pdf)). Corrected: the start year 2012 is not confirmed. A search summary says the 90% target took effect on 1 July 2024, and the earlier 31-day target was 85% (SNIPPET, [health targets](https://www.health.govt.nz/monitoring-statistics/system-monitoring/health-targets)).
- **Government statement (READ):** "The Government has set a target of 90% of patients receiving cancer management within 31 days of the decision to treat. Regular reporting at district level against health targets shows what progress is being made and how districts compare across the country." Source: https://www.health.govt.nz/system/files/2026-08/Cabinet-Paper-Assuring-New-Zealand-s-Performance-on-Cancer.pdf (READ via highlights; the PDF itself could not be parsed by WebFetch).
- **Results (SNIPPET):**
  - Health NZ Q4 2024/25 factsheet: 83.5% (Q4 2023/24) to 86.3% (Q4 2024/25), target 90% by 2030. URL: https://www.tewhatuora.govt.nz/assets/Corporate-information/Planning-and-performance/Health-targets/Quarter-4-2024-25/Health-targets-Factsheets.pdf
  - June 2026 ministerial release: 86.2% in a recent quarter (quarter inferred; secondary source, [source needed]).
- **Equity (READ):** Cabinet paper says "significant inequities in outcomes persist, particularly for Māori and Pacific peoples and people living in high deprivation." It warns that gaps can widen "even when aggregate performance indicators appear stable."
- **Equity evidence, breast cancer (SNIPPET):**
  - Lancet Regional Health Western Pacific, 2024: about 16,000 women with early breast cancer, four urban regions. Māori and Pacific women more likely to wait over 31 days for surgery. Gaps persisted after FCT. https://doaj.org/article/c652af64ece946c8ad68fb3ef8dd5b27 (DOI not verified; [source needed] for DOI)
  - Cancer Causes & Control, 2025: about 20,000 women. Most deprived women more likely to wait over 31 days. FCT's post-introduction reduction was small and not significant. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10552-025-02032-0
- **Māori national 31-day result:** not found. One iwi partnership board figure (95.97% of Māori within 31 days) uses diagnosis as start, so is not comparable (SNIPPET, [source needed]).
- **Outcome evidence:** No survival or stage-at-diagnosis evaluation of FCT found.

### 3b. Health target district reporting: P1

- Health NZ publishes quarterly factsheets and district dashboards split by ethnicity (SNIPPET, [Health NZ factsheet](https://www.tewhatuora.govt.nz/assets/Corporate-information/Planning-and-performance/Health-targets/Quarter-4-2024-25/Health-targets-Factsheets.pdf)). Example: Pacific people in Wairarapa 0.0% within 31 days (one quarter; SNIPPET, [source needed]).
- This is the strongest "measure the total clock publicly" example in this set. It measures decision-to-treat, not referral-to-treatment, so it misses the GP-to-decision part (P1 gap).

## Transferable to Finland

- **Measure the full clock, publish by hospital and district.** NZ publishes a 31-day target by district every quarter (READ). Finland should define start (GP referral or suspicion) and end (first treatment) and publish both. Ontario DAP data shows time to diagnosis and time to treatment can move separately, so track both (see 1a).
- **Use a one-stop fast lane, but measure what it moves.** Ontario's lung DAP cut time to treatment by 8.5 days, not time to diagnosis (READ, see 1a). Design the Finnish fast lane around imaging plus specialist in one visit, and check whether it is open to people who enter via public GP (the two-speed lens). Ontario sources did not report entry by income; that is a gap to check.
- **Make stage a registrar task fed by standard pathology reports.** Ontario uses pathology e-reporting plus registrar Collaborative Staging, yet still has 11.2% NSCLC unstaged (READ, see 1c). Australia's RD-Stage gets 88% to 97% for common cancers but 72% for lung (SNIPPET, see 2c, [source needed]). Plan for lung and colorectal to be hardest.
- **Treat missing stage as an equity signal.** Ontario's unstaged NSCLC patients were older, poorer, more comorbid, and died faster (READ, see 1c). Report stage completeness by income and deprivation, not just overall.
- **Require equity breakdowns in the same report as the national target.** NZ hits 86% overall but Māori and Pacific gaps persist (READ, see 3a). Finland should show the 58% stage gap and the wait gap by income in one dashboard.
- **Accountability without proven money link.** Ontario uses quarterly reviews of regional programmes (SNIPPET, see 1b). Its funding link is disputed (SNIPPET, see 1b). Quarterly review is cheap to copy; tying money to it is not proven.
