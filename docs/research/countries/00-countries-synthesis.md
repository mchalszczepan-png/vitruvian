# What other countries did on waits, fast lanes and stage – synthesis

Date: 2026-11-14. Research only. Sources: the five country files in this folder (01–05), written by Haiku sub-agents. Links below are copied from those files; each row names the file that holds the full source. UK and Sweden are not repeated here; see `docs/research/context/03-what-others-tried.md`.

Problems: **P1** = nobody measures the total cancer wait. **P3** = no fast lane for suspected cancer from public GP. **P4** = stage missing for most registry cases. **Lens** = two-speed entry (lower income starts in the slow public route).

Tags: **READ** = page or abstract read. **SNIPPET** = search summary only. **UNVERIFIED** = memory or secondary. **[source needed]** = claim kept, no source after two searches. "Our reading" = our judgement, not a sourced claim.

**Bottom line** (our reading): many countries *did* these things. Almost none *proved* they shortened the total wait, moved stage earlier or narrowed income gaps. What is proven is narrower: public wait monitoring is doable at national scale, and stage completeness above 90% is reachable with registrars or structured feeds. A fast lane on its own did not equalise stage anywhere it was tested.

## Top 5 lessons for package A (GP fast lane + risk prompt, county wait view, auto stage report)

Our reading throughout this section.

1. **Wait view: copy Denmark's coded timestamps, show every step plus the total, and split by route and income.** Denmark publishes quarterly per region from coded points in the patient register (READ, [Q4 2025 report](https://cdn1.gopublic.dk/sundhedsdatastyrelsen/Media/639089831953237646/monitorering_kraeft_q4_2025.pdf)). Norway found decision → treatment is the main delay ([Nilssen 2022](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12885-022-09306-9), READ). Australia found diagnosis → treatment most missed (SNIPPET, [source needed]); Ontario: [source needed]. So the clock must run to first treatment, not stop at diagnosis. NZ shows the aggregate can rise while Māori and deprived patients still wait longer ([Cabinet paper](https://www.health.govt.nz/system/files/2026-08/Cabinet-Paper-Assuring-New-Zealand-s-Performance-on-Cancer.pdf), READ). Put the equity split on the same screen as the target.

2. **GP fast lane: build it like Catalonia, not like a clinic.** One electronic referral form in the shared record, agreed red-flag symptoms, a named hospital per health centre, first test within 2 weeks ([3cat](https://www.3cat.cat/3catinfo/amplien-a-11-tumors-el-circuit-de-diagnostic-rapid-per-facilitar-lacces-al-tractament-en-30-dies/noticia-amp/3387454/), READ). One-stop clinics alone (Ontario) did not shorten time to diagnosis ([Habbous 2021](https://doi.org/10.4103/atm.atm_283_20), READ). Finland has no shared form or fixed GP–hospital link for suspected cancer today ([source needed]).

3. **A fast lane alone does not fix two-speed entry: measure who gets in.** Ireland's public prostate clinics did not cut late stage or the deprivation gap ([PMC13602753](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13602753/), READ). In Norway, low income and comorbidity were linked to not being in a pathway ([Nilssen 2020](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12885-020-06979-y), SNIPPET). Package A must log entry route and area income for every fast-lane referral and flag crowd-out. The same message appears in the Sweden and UK notes ([context/03-what-others-tried.md](context/03-what-others-tried.md)).

4. **Risk prompt: support, not the cure. Pair it with training and track uptake.** Danish GPs given direct CT access only got faster if they took the training (23 vs 66 days, [Guldbrandt 2015](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12885-015-1941-2), READ). GP "readiness to investigate" correlates with survival, but Baltic GPs act more and still have lower survival ([Rose 2015](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2014-007212), READ; [Rosendahl 2025](https://doi.org/10.1080/02813432.2025.2451653), READ). Pitch the prompt as one part of the loop, measured by referral rate and time-to-referral, not as proof of better survival.

5. **Auto stage report: attach stage to a step that already happens, and treat missing stage as an equity flag.** Countries above 90% use paid registrars (Netherlands) or a stage form filled at the tumour-board meeting (Belgium: 90.4% of patients discussed, not reconfirmed; see the table). Pathology e-feeds help but leave lung hard (Ontario 11.2% unstaged in NSCLC, Australia lung 72%). Missing stage clusters in older, poorer, sicker patients in Ontario (READ, above). Denmark's lung TNM completeness is lower at age 80+ and with high comorbidity ([CLEP.S33315](https://doi.org/10.2147/CLEP.S33315), SNIPPET). Show completeness by area income. Never code missing as "no spread": the Nordic registries count missing stage as N0M0, which inflates some shares ([PMC11332511](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11332511/), READ). Local example: Pirkanmaa's clinical notification rose from 42% (2022) to 80% (2024) ([registry article](https://syoparekisteri.fi/2025/06/26/yhteistyo-toi-parannusta-syoparekisteri-ilmoituksiin/), SNIPPET; detail in [context/13-pirkanmaa-stage-effects.md](../context/13-pirkanmaa-stage-effects.md)). This is a local trend, not proof of a stage effect (our reading).

## Corrections and conflicts for our own summary

- **Denmark/Sweden "~96% lung stage"** (RESEARCH-SUMMARY §3, problem 4): **not confirmed.** Verified: Danish lung registry stage *validity* 90.1% ([Gouliaev 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lungcan.2024.107527), READ); Nordic table shows 75–95% TNM availability for most cancers ([PMC11332511](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11332511/), READ), country mapping for lung unclear. Rephrase to "Nordic neighbours record stage for 75–95% of most cancers" until the table is checked.
- **Norway target:** the national pathway target was met only in 2020 and 2021 ([Helsedirektoratet](https://www.helsedirektoratet.no/statistikk/kvalitetsindikatorer/kreft-behandling-og-overlevelse/pakkeforl%C3%B8p-for-26-organspesifikke-kreftformer), READ). Not met since 2022. Publication is paused until an NPR data error is corrected (READ). Do not quote "70% met" as current.
- **Finland "fines providers" (clash, corrected):** the 2025 OECD Finland profile ([PDF](https://cancer-inequalities.jrc.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/docs/ccp2025/ec-oecd-fi-2024-1666-en.pdf), READ) says Finland has a six-week target from suspected cancer referral to first treatment, with waiting times monitored, and a plan to add stage and waiting times to the cancer quality registry. The **fines** wording is only in the 2024 OECD highlights ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1787/14fdc89a-en), READ highlights), and that detail is [source needed] in file 05. This clashes with our finding that nobody publishes cancer waits. Likely the general care-guarantee supervision, not cancer-specific. **Check before the pitch** – a judge may quote it.
- **OECD says Finland plans to add stage and waiting times to the cancer quality registry** ([OECD Finland profile PDF](https://cancer-inequalities.jrc.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/docs/ccp2025/ec-oecd-fi-2024-1666-en.pdf), READ). Good for us: package A fits a stated national plan.
- **NZ:** current headline is the 31-day target ([health targets](https://www.health.govt.nz/monitoring-statistics/system-monitoring/health-targets), SNIPPET); status of the older 62-day indicator unclear (SNIPPET).
- **Finland is absent from ICBP interval and GP vignette studies** ([Weller 2018, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2018-023870); [Rose 2015, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2014-007212)). Any Finnish interval data would be new.
- **Pirkanmaa:** the 42% → 80% figures are a notification rate, not spread data. National spread data was 41.9% in 2024 (file 13).
