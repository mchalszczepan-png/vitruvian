# Europe (other): Estonia, Ireland, Slovenia, Catalonia, France, Italy

Scope: cancer waiting times, fast lanes for suspected cancer, and stage-at-diagnosis completeness.
Problem key: **P1** = nobody measures total cancer wait. **P3** = no fast lane from public GP. **P4** = stage missing for ~58% of Finnish registry cases ([source needed]). **Lens** = two-speed entry (lower-income people start in the slow public GP route).

Tags: **READ** = I read the page or abstract. **SNIPPET** = only the search summary. **UNVERIFIED** = memory or secondary, not checked. **[source needed]** = claim kept, no URL found after two searches.
Acronyms: NCCP = National Cancer Control Programme (Ireland). RAC / RAPC = Rapid Access Clinic / Rapid Access Prostate Clinic. NCRI = National Cancer Registry Ireland. DPOR = Slovenian national cancer control programme. DRC = Catalan rapid cancer diagnosis circuit. PDTA = Italian regional care pathway. PNGLA = Italian national waiting-list plan. AIRTUM = Italian cancer registry network. INCa = French National Cancer Institute. PPV = positive predictive value. KPI = key performance indicator.

## Ireland

### Rapid Access Clinics (RAC) and targets
- Eight designated cancer centres run rapid access clinics for breast (symptomatic breast disease, SBD), lung (RALC) and prostate (RAPC) (SNIPPET, HSE parliamentary reply: https://www.hse.ie/eng/about/personalpq/pq/2025-pq-responses/april-2025/pq-17507-25-padraig-rice.pdf).
- Targets (SNIPPET, Irish Times 9 Nov 2026: https://www.irishtimes.com/health/2026/11/09/patients-in-west-and-northwest-likely-to-wait-longer-for-critical-cancer-screening-services/):
  - Urgent breast: 95% seen within 10 working days. Non-urgent breast: within 12 weeks.
  - Lung: 96% within 10 working days.
  - Prostate: 90% within 20 working days.
  - The NCCP KPI for breast is 95% (SNIPPET, cancer.ie scorecard: https://www.cancer.ie/sites/default/files/2025-10/National_Cancer_Strategy_scorecard_October2025.pdf).
- Results (SNIPPET):
  - 2024: 76.4% of symptomatic breast attendances seen within 10 working days (21,426 of 28,050) (SNIPPET, HSE parliamentary replies, exact PDF [source needed]). The Irish Cancer Society gives 76.3% for March 2024 to February 2025 (SNIPPET, [source needed]).
  - 2025 to date: 69.4% (8,277 of 11,926) (SNIPPET, [source needed]). Conflict: another HSE table in the same search reads 66.9% for January to June 2025, so do not quote this figure until checked.
  - An Irish Examiner piece says 61% of women with urgent breast referrals were seen within two weeks, but the year is unclear (https://www.irishexaminer.com/news/arid-41880889.html). Do not cite without checking.
  - Scorecard says the latest published KPI report covers 2023 (SNIPPET, cancer.ie scorecard above). It conflicts with the 2024 operational figure, so the official report lags.
  - Dublin/North East and the West are both behind targets; the West is worse (SNIPPET, Irish Times above).
- Reporting: public KPI reports exist (HSE National Performance Report; NCCP KPI reports) ([source needed]). I did not open any current one (not retrieved).

### Equity and the two-tier question (most relevant to our lens)
- READ, [Haugh et al. 2026, ESMO Real World Data and Digital Oncology 14:100781](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13602753/) (prostate, RAPC introduced 2009, data 2000-2022, Irish registry-linked data):
  - 33% of cases were diagnosed in private hospitals, 24% in public hospitals, 44% in cancer centres (Table 1, overall).
  - Deprived men use the public pathway more; less deprived men use private services more (intro text).
  - Late stage (III-IV) did not fall after RAPC. It rose from about 18% before to about 24% after (Table 1: III 11% to 17.2%, IV 7.4% to 7%).
  - Stage unknown fell from 16.6% (pre) to 3.8% (post) (Table 1, Discussion).
  - Authors' conclusion: "centralisation of diagnostic services alone is insufficient in achieving equity in PCa detection."
  - Limit: deprivation denominators were not available, so the study compares trends, not rates.
- Our reading: a public fast lane did not, on its own, equalise stage for prostate. This is the strongest local warning for our two-speed lens.
- I did not find a breast or lung equivalent of this study (not searched in time).

### Stage data: NCRI
- Tumour registration officers (now called Cancer Data Registrars) are based in hospitals and collect cases from pathology and other sources (SNIPPET, https://www.ncri.ie/node/184).
- Stage needs follow-up information and can arrive months later (SNIPPET, https://ncri.ie/en/understanding-cancer-data-reporting-timelines).
- Case capture completeness for invasive cancers to end 2007 was over 96% (SNIPPET, older NCRI atlas, https://www.ncri.ie/node/184). This is capture, not stage.
- A 2016 TCD thesis found better staging capture in hospitals with better ICT access (SNIPPET, https://publications.scss.tcd.ie/theses/diss/2016/TCD-SCSS-DISSERTATION-2016-018.pdf).
- Overall stage completeness rate for NCRI is not confirmed.

## Estonia

### Did it
- Cancer Control Plan 2021-2030 approved May 2021 (READ, tai.ee: https://tai.ee/en/tervis-ja-heaolu/cancer-control). The page gives no waiting-time or stage targets and no monitoring indicators.
- Mid-term evaluation (Causalis and Praxis Center): the Baltic Times reports implementation is progressing too slowly (SNIPPET, https://www.baltictimes.com/report__cancer_control_action_plan_implementation_progressing_too_slowly/). The evaluation report (Estonian PDF, https://www.sm.ee/sites/default/files/documents/2026-04/Eesti%20V%C3%A4hit%C3%B5rje%20tegevuskava%202021%E2%80%932030%20vahehindamine_L%C3%95PPRAPORT_0.pdf) was found but not read in full. The earlier split "13% systematically implemented, 41% partly, 45% not yet" is **not confirmed** by any retrieved source (corrected; [source needed]).

### Stage and registry
- Registry run by the National Institute for Health Development (NIHD). It is population-based and publishes stage reports (READ, tai.ee).
- 2024: 9,926 primary cancer diagnoses (READ, tai.ee).
- Reporting is mandatory for physicians, with clinical notifications and laboratory notifications from pathology and haematology labs. The registry checks its database against two big hospitals and queries providers (SNIPPET, en.tai.ee registry page: https://en.tai.ee/en/r-and-d/registers/estonian-cancer-registry).
- Death-certificate-only cases exist in 2023 data, a sign of incomplete notification (SNIPPET, NIHD 2026 report, [source needed]).
- Early-stage rates in 2018 (SNIPPET, National Audit Office, Dec 2021, https://www.riigikontroll.ee/sites/default/files/arhivaalid/2539/RKTR_2539_2-1.4_2281_005-1.pdf): cervical 29%, colorectal 35%, breast 49%. The NAO PDF itself was binary and unreadable with my tools.
- Automatic stage feed from the national e-health record (ENHIS / Tervise infosüsteem) into the cancer registry: **not found**. An electronic notification system was being designed (TalTech thesis, 2023, SNIPPET, [source needed]). The screening registry does get automated feeds over X-Road, but that is a separate register ([source needed]).

### Waits
- Breast cancer diagnosis: median 8 days in 2016 billing data; West Estonia longest, 43 days via GP route (SNIPPET, TalTech thesis 2019, [source needed]).
- NAO 2021 says delays happen at both GP and specialist level, and long exam waits add to treatment delay (SNIPPET, NAO PDF above).

Five-year survival 61% and data-infrastructure gaps are mentioned only in the search summary (SNIPPET, [source needed]).

## Catalonia (Spain)

### Did it
- Cancer Fast-track Programme, launched 2005 for breast, colorectal and lung; bladder and prostate added 2007 (SNIPPET, [Prades et al., BJC 2011](https://doi.org/10.1038/bjc.2011.308)).
- Now called the DRC circuit. Expanded from 5 to 11 tumour types (announced 31 Dec 2025): adds gastro-oesophageal, pancreas, kidney, ovary, uterus/cervix, skin (READ, 3cat: https://www.3cat.cat/3catinfo/amplien-a-11-tumors-el-circuit-de-diagnostic-rapid-per-facilitar-lacces-al-tractament-en-30-dies/noticia-amp/3387454/).
- Who can refer: primary and community care, ASSIR (sexual and reproductive health), screening programmes, hospital ER, outpatient specialists (READ, 3cat).
- How it works (READ, 3cat):
  - One electronic DRC referral form in the shared clinical record. Any professional who spots suspicion starts it.
  - Consensus signs and symptoms per tumour, and tests to do in primary care before referral.
  - Each primary-care team is linked to a reference hospital.
  - If suspicion is ruled out, the patient goes back to primary care.
  - First urgent or preferential test and report within 2 weeks of referral.
  - Target: about 30 days on average from suspicion to diagnostic confirmation and start of treatment.
- The 2025 article gives no referral numbers and no achieved times.

### Evidence
- Source: Prades et al., "Implementing a Cancer Fast-track Programme between primary and specialised care in Catalonia (Spain): a mixed methods study", BJC 2011. [DOI 10.1038/bjc.2011.308](https://doi.org/10.1038/bjc.2011.308) (metadata verified by lookup; the full text was behind a login).
- SNIPPET numbers: about half of new breast, lung and colorectal patients were diagnosed via the fast track; mean days from primary-care detection to treatment in 2009: breast 32, colorectal 30, lung 37 (same DOI, SNIPPET). The detection rate fell over the period.
- Pre-2011 targets (SNIPPET, old slides): 80% of lung, breast and colorectal diagnoses within 15 days (2012 slides, [source needed]). Current targets are not confirmed.
- PPV: no PPV found for the DRC circuit. A 2019 PPV study covers SIDIAP primary-care records, not the referral circuit (SNIPPET, [source needed]).
- Stage and survival outcomes: not found.

## France

### Did it
- Plan Cancer 2026-2030 (second roadmap of the 2021-2030 strategy), unveiled by INCa on 4 Feb 2026. About EUR 1.7 bn for 2026-2030 (SNIPPET, ICCP: https://iccp-portal.org/news/france-launches-new-2026-2030-cancer-action-plan-world-cancer-day).
- Diagnosis goals: faster access to diagnosis, accelerated entry routes for poor-prognosis cancers, and a digital one-stop guichet to help patients organise their pathway (SNIPPET, cancer.fr roadmap summary, [source needed]).
- Auvergne-Rhone-Alpes regional roadmap 2026-2030 names territorial disparities and cites a "Cancerdiag" one-stop diagnostic service as a tool. I could not find what Cancerdiag is or how it performed (SNIPPET, ARS AURA: https://www.auvergne-rhone-alpes.ars.sante.fr/cancers-une-nouvelle-feuille-de-route-regionale-pour-2026-2030).
- A national cancer registry under INCa supervision was voted in June 2025 to document social and territorial inequalities (SNIPPET, [source needed]).

### Measurement
- INCa regional study of delays, 8 regions (breast and lung): breast access-to-diagnosis average ranged from 11.3 days (Auvergne) to 25.4 days (Nord-Pas-de-Calais). Lung differences were smaller (SNIPPET, Le Quotidien du Medecin: https://www.lequotidiendumedecin.fr/actus-medicales/sante-publique/cancer-du-sein-les-delais-de-prise-en-charge-varient-du-simple-au-double). Year not confirmed.
- A second phase (colon and prostate, 13 regions) was reported in progress (SNIPPET, year unclear, [source needed]).
- Older INCa figure: prostate average time to diagnosis 36.5 days (SNIPPET, 2015 study, UNVERIFIED for year, [source needed]).
- Francim (regional registries) data on delays not retrieved.

### Evidence
- No outcome evaluation found for Cancerdiag or for the 2026 pathways. Dispositif d'annonce (structured diagnosis-announcement support) not checked for outcomes (not searched in time; UNVERIFIED).

## Italy

### Did it
- National waiting-list platform (AGENAS), first periodic bulletin: tracks 14 first-visit types and 22 diagnostic tests in public and accredited private services (SNIPPET, Quotidiano Sanita: https://www.quotidianosanita.it/?p=152637).
- H1 2026: oncology visits 95.2% delivered within guarantee time; only oncology visits met the 90% standard. Strong territorial inequality. Five regions below 70% overall (SNIPPET, same source).
- PNGLA 2019-2021 surgery indicator: breast, lung, colorectal and prostate cancer surgery within 30 days of booking. Not always met (SNIPPET, [Quotidiano Sanita, Agenas data](https://www.quotidianosanita.it/studi-e-analisi/tumori-da-nord-a-sud-tempi-di-attesa-ancora-a-macchia-di-leopardo-ecco-quanto-bisogna-aspettare-per-fare-un-intervento/)). The Agenas definition counts from booking date; other local monitoring counts from list entry, so compare only within one definition.
- PNGLA 2024-2026 was pending when a late-2024 report was written; adoption not confirmed ([source needed]).
- PDTA (regional care pathways): no source found (not searched successfully).

### Stage data
- AIRTUM 2013 presentation: about 25% of pooled cases had missing stage, roughly 114,000 cases. Old figure, table hard to read (SNIPPET, https://www.registri-tumori.it/PDF/Bolzano2013/21_03_2013/mattino/01_Buzzoni.pdf).
- Population study of 31 registries (47% of population, data to 2017) reports stage-specific cure for breast and colorectal (SNIPPET, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10374783).
- Current AIRTUM stage-missing share: not found.

### Evidence
- No cancer diagnosis-time metric found. No outcome evaluation of PDTA or fast lanes found.

## Transferable to Finland

- **Measure the whole pathway, by entry route.** Ireland's prostate study splits cases by public vs private hospital and by deprivation (READ, [Haugh et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13602753/)). We can do the same for our lens: GP entry, time to first test, time to treatment, split by income or insurance. Catalonia's DRC has a clock for each step (2 weeks to first test, about 30 days to treatment), so the steps are measurable (READ, 3cat).
- **A fast lane alone may not equalise stage.** Ireland's RAPC did not cut late stage and did not remove the deprivation gap (READ, [Haugh et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13602753/)). Our fix should address entry (who gets referred) and stage capture, not only the speed of the clinic.
- **Make stage capture part of the referral and treatment record.** Ireland's unknown-stage share fell from 16.6% to 3.8% after centralised prostate diagnosis (READ, [Haugh et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13602753/), Table 1). The mechanism is not stated in what I read, so test it before copying.
- **Use one shared electronic referral form that any clinician can trigger.** Catalonia's DRC does this, with a named reference hospital per primary-care team (READ, 3cat). It links directly to problem 3.
- **Registry notification from labs and clinics.** Estonia and Ireland rely on mandatory notification from pathology labs and clinicians and on registrars who query providers (SNIPPET, see Estonia and NCRI sections; [source needed] for the Irish notification route). Finland's gap is hospital notifications, so the lab-notification route is worth checking against our own system (not verified here).
- **No one publishes a total cancer wait.** Italy publishes guarantee-time compliance per service ([Quotidiano Sanita](https://www.quotidianosanita.it/?p=152637)), and Ireland publishes per-clinic KPIs ([cancer.ie scorecard](https://www.cancer.ie/sites/default/files/2025-10/National_Cancer_Strategy_scorecard_October2025.pdf)), but neither measures the full cancer wait. That is the open space for P1.
